FollowUp asks the owner what a new employee would ask

Today FollowUp only knows a business's name and trade. So it can't tell a customer the price of a cleaning, it doesn't know which areas the owner covers, and it has to guess who isn't a customer. This design fills that gap with short questions: at most one a day, and one tap to answer or skip. Answers are kept for this business only and used every time FollowUp sorts a message or writes a reply. Nothing trains a model.

Example business: Brightwater Dental. All names and prices are made up.

Everything FollowUp has been told is in one place, in plain words, and each item is one tap to change. It sits in Settings next to "Write like me" and follows the same promise: your business only, and you can remove it.
The first seven questions

In this order, one a day at most

  1. What do people ask you for most? (choices from the trade)
  2. Roughly what does that cost?
  3. Which areas do you cover?
  4. Who writes to you that isn't a customer?
  5. When are you open, or when do you work?
  6. What should FollowUp always check with you first? (discounts, same-day, refunds…)
  7. How do customers usually book: a call, a link, or a reply?

After these, there's no daily question. FollowUp only asks when a real message needs something it doesn't know (frame 3), or when the owner's taps show a pattern (frame 4).

Not annoying

The rules

  • Never a notification, email or badge for a question. It waits on the screen.
  • Never above a customer who needs the owner.
  • Phone: only when the list is done. Desktop: the side column.
  • At most one a day. A skip comes back once, a week later.
  • Off with one switch in Settings.
  • Written as a colleague would ask: plain words, no "AI", no sparkles.
Trust

What happens to the answers

  • Kept for this business only, and shown in full in Settings.
  • Used when FollowUp sorts a message and writes a reply. Prices still wait for the owner's OK (A-070 stays).
  • Never used to train a model, and never shared.
  • Included in "Download everything" and removed with the business.
Weak spots, honestly

What I'm unsure of

  • Desktop question beside a busy list might still pull attention. If so, desktop could also wait until the list is done.
  • Prices change. The answer is dated, and after 90 days FollowUp asks "Still $120–180?"
  • Seven questions may be too many for some trades. The first three do most of the work.
