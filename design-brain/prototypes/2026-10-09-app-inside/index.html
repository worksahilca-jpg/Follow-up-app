The map · so new features fit

Three places, one window, and seven slots for everything else.

You asked for a plan before building, so new features fit. The skeleton stays fixed. Every feature, today's and the ones on the way, goes into one of seven slots. Nothing gets squeezed in, and the app never grows a fifth tab.

  1. The owner must decide now → a card in Today, one at a time.
  2. It's about one customer → a short block in that customer's side panel ("Details" on the phone).
  3. It's a way to find people → a tab in Customers (only when it has people) or a filter.
  4. It shows what FollowUp did → a number on Results, real records only, beside last week.
  5. It's how FollowUp works, or a connection → a section or row in the Settings window.
  6. It's one task with a start and an end → a small window (a sheet on the phone).
  7. It happened while you were away → an alert that opens straight to the customer.

The strategy · version 2

Four places, each built on an app you already use.

Each place has one job. Each works like an app the owner opens every day, so there's nothing to learn. We borrow how those apps work, not how they look: copying a screen is against our rules, and the pattern is what makes it easy.

  • The Wispr Flow lesson. Wispr works inside the apps you already use; its own app is a quiet hub (your stats, your words, your style), and setup has you try it in a real app. For us: a phone alert opens straight to the reply with Send, replies go out from your own Gmail or WhatsApp, and setup ends on a real or practice customer, never a tour.
  • Results is the "Numbers" page we already have, hidden under Settings today, back in the menu. Menu: Today · Customers · Results · Settings, each with a word.
  • Every screen keeps 10 rules from the Laws of UX: one job, each fact said once, one black button, a word on every icon, machinery behind "Details", real numbers or none, and the desk follows the phone.
