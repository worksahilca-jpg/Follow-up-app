## What is interesting

*What you actually see. Observation, not evaluation. Be specific enough that someone

## Interaction principle

*What happens on hover, click, load, error, empty, and slow network. Often the most
valuable field and the most often skipped.*

## Whose user is this?

*Who uses this product, how often, how expert, on what device, under what pressure — and
how that compares to a FollowUp user (an owner or small team, often on a phone, often
interrupted, not a software person). A pattern that's great for a power user in a tool
all day may be hostile here.*

## What FollowUp should NOT copy

*Never leave this blank. There is always something — a brand-specific flourish, a density
that doesn't suit our user, a pattern that depends on their scale or their conventions,
an aesthetic that conflicts with our principles.*

## Possible FollowUp application

*A concrete, specific idea. "The lead list could adopt X so that Y." Not "we could be
more like this."*
