# References — Dashboards

Overview screens, metrics, at-a-glance status, product home screens.

## What to look for here

- **What gets the top-left.** The single most important thing on a dashboard reveals what
  the product believes its job is. For FollowUp that should be *what needs you now*, not
  *how many leads you have*.
- **Metrics that drive action vs. metrics that flatter.** A number with no next step is
  decoration. Look for dashboards where each figure implies something to do.
- **How emptiness is handled** on day one, before there's data.
- **Density and scan order** — where the eye goes first, second, third.
- **Restraint with charts.** Most excellent dashboards have fewer charts than you remember.

## The FollowUp-specific question

Our dashboard's job is to answer *"what needs me right now, and what has FollowUp already
handled?"* in under five seconds, often on a phone. Judge every dashboard reference
against that, not against how impressive it looks in a screenshot.

## Anti-patterns to notice and name

Six chart colors; metric cards with no next action; vanity counters; a grid of equal-weight
tiles with no hierarchy; "welcome back, [Name]!" occupying prime space.
