## What is interesting

- **Linear:** a solid circle with three diagonal cuts across one side. One shape plus negative space, and it reads
  as speed.
- **Attio (a CRM):** two leaning, rounded forms, outlined: a tall one and a smaller one to its lower right.
- **Intercom:** a rounded square holding five upright bars whose bottoms curve into a smile. The app-icon shape is
  the logo.
- **Stripe:** a heavy letter S. **Notion:** an N on a paper-like block with a hand-cut edge. **Amplitude:** an A
  in a circle beside the name.
- **Framer:** an abstract F built from three flat pieces, a parallelogram, a square and a triangle.
- **Todoist:** three stacked check-like chevrons in a rounded square: a list getting done.
- **HubSpot:** a node with spokes, a hub.
- **Zapier:** the name is the logo. **Duolingo:** the owl, a character.

## Visual principle

1. **The best ones are one simple idea plus negative space.** Linear, Framer and Todoist all still read at 16 px.
2. **Letter marks are the SaaS default** (Stripe, Notion, Framer, Amplitude). A mark built on an F joins that
   crowd. Framer already owns "an abstract F from flat forward pieces", and FollowUp's current leaves and option B
   (straight edges) sit in its neighbourhood.
3. **The ones with meaning show what the product does:**
   - Todoist: tasks getting done;
   - Intercom: a friendly conversation;
   - HubSpot: things connecting;
   - Linear: forward motion.
4. **All of them work in plain black.** Colour is added later. This matches FollowUp's monochrome rule.
5. **On a phone the mark lives in a rounded square** (Intercom, Todoist, Zapier, Duolingo). It has to fill about
   60% of it and read in white on the brand colour.

## Whose user is this?

Mostly software buyers and teams in tools all day. FollowUp's owner is not a software person and meets the mark in
three places: a browser tab, a phone home screen, and the top of a notification email. The mark has to say
"messages" there without being read closely.

## What FollowUp could learn

- **None of the eleven shows two people, a message and a reply.** That's open ground, and it is the founder's pick,
  H (A-061). Draw it as a conversation: one message, then the answer on the other side. Drawn as a big shape and a
  small leaning shape, it looks like Attio's mark, and Attio is a CRM. Keep it upright and horizontal, like chat.
- **Check against Todoist:** stacked horizontal shapes in a dark square. Each H message keeps one sharp corner that
  points to who spoke. That makes them messages, not chevrons or bars.
- **Keep one idea and let the gap do the work,** as Linear does. The gap between the two messages is the moment
  FollowUp covers.
- **Don't add a letter.** The F lives in the name beside the mark.

**Must not copy:** any of these shapes, their proportions or their app-icon treatment. This file keeps principles,
not geometry.
