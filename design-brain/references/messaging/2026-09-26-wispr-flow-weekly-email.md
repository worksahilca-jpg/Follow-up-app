## What it does (B = search summary of a named source)

- **A highlights card first:** streak, total words, average speed, apps used, in that order. (B: Wispr Flow Help
  Center, "Your Usage tab")
- **Big single numbers**, each with one comparison (speed as a percentile against other users). (B)
- **Where** you used it, your **peak time**, and "superlatives". (B)
- **Monday team email:** leaderboard rank, a celebratory version for the top 5, and one button, "See
  leaderboard". (B: Wispr Flow Help Center, Pro/Enterprise FAQ)
- A branded frame: imagery in the background, the logo, and company links at the end. (Founder's description.)

## What FollowUp takes

The order: highlight, then big numbers with one comparison each, then the one action with one button, then where
customers wrote from, the busiest time, and a branded footer.

## What FollowUp doesn't take, and why

- **Streaks, leaderboards and percentiles.** The founder said "skip the streaks", and the Duolingo study
  (`research/ux-patterns/2026-09-26-duolingo-progress-and-completion.md`) already ruled these out.
- **Stock photography.** The "photo" is FollowUp's own landing wash (A-032). A stock photo of a smiling
  tradesperson is a startup template and isn't ours.
- **Blog links.** FollowUp has no blog. The footer links only to pages that exist.
