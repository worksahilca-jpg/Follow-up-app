## What is interesting (from the sources, not from screens)

- **A total first.** The home screen shows a "My balances" total, and the owner can customise the Home screen and that
  total (Scotiabank feature page).
- **Advice in its own tab, with a preview on Home.** Personal insights, spending tracking and budgeting sit under an
  "Advice+" tab in the main menu. Insights, alerts and recommendations "may also appear on the app's home screen" (Scotiabank help page).
- **Help everywhere.** Since the 2019 redesign, a question-mark icon opens a Help Centre with FAQs and a search (press, 2019).
- **Shortcuts.** The 2019 redesign added shortcuts for navigation, and 2021 added search across products, information and
  shortcuts (press).
- **Accessibility.** Dynamic font sizing and screen-reader support (press; app listing).
- **Built on an in-house design system** (called "Canvas" in the 2019 press). Its documentation is not public.
- **Rated about 4.7 from about a million iOS ratings** (third-party tracker). The app serves Canadian accounts only.

## UX principle (provisional, until screens are seen)

A bank app is trusted because:
- one number tells you where you stand;
- the few things you do most are one tap away;
- everything else is a level down and grouped by what it is;
- help is always in the same place.

## Whose user is this?

A broad Canadian public, many of them not technical, checking quickly and often on a phone, with money at stake. That
is close to a FollowUp owner, closer than Linear's or Superhuman's users. The difference: a bank user mostly *looks*
(balances), while a FollowUp owner must *decide* (send, edit, or not). FollowUp's "one number" has to lead straight to
the one decision.

## What FollowUp could learn

- Today's headline already works like "My balances" ("15 customers need you"). Keep one number, and make it tappable,
  so it leads straight into the first decision.
- "What FollowUp did" could work like Advice+: a short preview on Today, and the full story one level down (part 5,
  decision 13).
- Help should be in the same place on every screen.

## What FollowUp should NOT copy

- Scotiabank's red, logo, wording, icons or any screen, recognisably.
- The density of a product menu: a bank sells many products; FollowUp has four places.
- Promotions or offers mixed into the home screen.
