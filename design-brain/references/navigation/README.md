# References — Navigation

Sidebars, top bars, information architecture, search, command palettes, wayfinding.

## What to look for here

- **How many top-level destinations** the product commits to, and what it refused to
  promote.
- **Where "where am I" is answered** — active states, breadcrumbs, page titles.
- **Search as navigation** — when a product replaces browsing with finding, and whether
  that works for a non-power user.
- **Mobile navigation** that isn't just the desktop sidebar in a drawer.
- **What's pinned vs. what's buried** — settings, account, help, notifications.

## The FollowUp-specific question

The shipped app has Dashboard, Leads, Pipeline, Workflows, Analytics, Activity, Settings.
That's seven, which is at the upper limit, and at least two of them ("Pipeline" vs.
"Leads", "Activity" vs. "Dashboard") may overlap in the user's mind. Look for references
that collapse related concepts cleanly, and note how they decided what deserved top level.

## Anti-patterns to notice and name

Command palettes as the only path to a feature; nav that grows one item per feature ship;
icon-only sidebars requiring memorization; hamburger menus on desktop; settings scattered
across three locations.
