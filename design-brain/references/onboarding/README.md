# References — Onboarding

Signup, account setup, integration connection, first-run experience, empty-to-full.

## What to look for here

- **Time to first value.** How many steps before the user sees something that's *theirs*.
- **What's deferred.** Excellent onboarding asks for almost nothing up front and collects
  the rest in context later.
- **Permission and OAuth moments** — how a product asks for access to something sensitive
  (an inbox, a social account) and earns the yes. This is directly our problem.
- **Progress honesty** — whether the progress indicator tells the truth.
- **The empty product.** What the app looks like in the ten minutes after setup, before
  data arrives.

## The FollowUp-specific question

FollowUp asks for a lot of trust very early: connect your inbox, your Instagram, your
phone number, and let us message your customers. **The onboarding's real job is earning
that permission, not collecting configuration.** Look for how references de-risk a big
ask — explaining what will and won't happen, showing before committing, making the exit
obvious.

## Anti-patterns to notice and name

Nine-step wizards; asking for everything before showing anything; fake progress bars;
"skip for now" that punishes you later; demanding a credit card before value; permission
requests with no explanation of scope.
