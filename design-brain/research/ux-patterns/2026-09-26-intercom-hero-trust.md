# Intercom: hero conversion and trust before delegation

**Date:** 2026-09-26 · **Asked by:** Sahil ("yes study Intercom next") · **Status:** STUDIED, proposals pending
approval

**Question (founder's reference strategy, one of the five deepest):** How does Intercom convert at the top of the
page, and how does it earn trust with customer conversations? Where should FollowUp do the same?

This is the conversion and trust half. The AI-inside-workflows half is `2026-09-26-intercom-ai-inside-workflows.md`.
intercom.com and fin.ai are blocked from this environment, so the sources are Intercom's own pages and help articles
as search results show them. These are principles only, not copied.

---

## What Intercom does

- **The hero is a chain:**
  - A category claim ("The #1 AI Agent for customer service").
  - Two actions: **View demo** (low commitment) and **Start free trial**.
  - Risk reversal said plainly ("14-day free trial, no credit card"), repeated on the trial pages, even in the
    button.
  - Proof right away: logos, and one outcome number measured by their own product (average resolution rate across
    their customers).
- **Proof is the product's own measurement.** It isn't "5-star rated". It's a number their system produces for every
  customer, with how many customers it's averaged over.
- **Trust comes from control before and after.**
  - "Full visual control over how it behaves, before it ever reaches a customer, and every time after."
  - You can preview an answer, batch-test it on real past questions, and simulate whole conversations, all before
    anything goes live.
- **When it isn't sure, it hands over instead of guessing.** Low confidence goes to a human. This is said as a
  promise, not buried as a setting.
- **It only uses what you gave it.** Answers come from your own content. You can opt out, and deletion has a stated
  time limit.
- **A self-serve trust center.** Every claim is backed by a document you can read without asking.

### The principles underneath

- **P1:** One primary action, one lower-commitment action, one line that removes the risk, then proof. In that order.
- **P2:** Show it on the buyer's own material before asking them to trust it: preview, then go live.
- **P3:** "When unsure, it asks" is the trust promise that matters most for AI touching customers.
- **P4:** Proof is a number the product measures itself, with its base (how many, over what period).
- **P5:** Trust claims link to evidence you can read.

## Where FollowUp stands (checked on main, 2026-09-26)

- **Hero:**
  - One primary action ✓ and the lower-commitment "See how it works" ✓ (A-049).
  - Risk reversal ✓ ("Free while in beta. No card. It follows up for you. Nothing sends without your OK.").
  - **No proof under it.** That's correct for now (A-023). There's no real number yet.
- **The demo "See how it works" scrolls to isn't one story.** It's product cards (Who needs you, the activity list,
  team, replies that sound like you). The reference strategy's demo is a sequence: Sarah asked about pricing →
  waiting 2 days → needs attention → suggested reply → review.
- **Promises:** four ("Stops", "Safe", "Honest", "Yours"). "Safe" only covers money: "It never talks about money
  without you." Our actual rule is wider: prices, dates, anything tense, and anything it isn't sure about waits for
  the owner. The broader and more reassuring truth isn't said.
- **Preview before live, partly there.**
  - In the app: every reply is held by default, and Settings has "See an example" on each rule (A-044).
  - Missing: a first moment that says "here's what FollowUp would have written to your last few customers; nothing
    was sent". The owner has to trust before they see.
- **Trust evidence ✓:** /security says plainly what we have and haven't done yet (Mercury study).

## Proposals (ranked)

1. **See it on your own customers first (P2).**
   - Right after connecting an inbox (onboarding), one screen: "Here's what FollowUp would write to your last 3
     customers. Nothing was sent." It shows the real drafts, each with Send, Edit or Skip.
   - This is both trust (preview before live) and the onboarding target we agreed (find a real conversation and act
     on it).
   - The landing page's trust section gets one matching line: "Before anything goes out, you see exactly what it
     would write, to your own customers."
2. **"See how it works" lands on one story, in order (P1, the reference strategy's Demo).**
   - The demo section tells Sarah's case as a short sequence: asked about a price → no reply for 2 days → FollowUp
     puts Sarah on Today → the reply it wrote → you review and send.
   - It's one card that reads top to bottom, replacing the scattered product cards as the first thing the visitor
     sees there.
3. **"When it isn't sure, it asks you" (P3).**
   - The "Safe" promise becomes "When it isn't sure, it asks you. Prices, dates and anything tense wait for your OK."
   - It stays true to the code: holds cover price, dates and tense conversations, and everything is held by default.
4. **A real number under the hero, only when it's real (P4, A-023).**
   - The slot is built but hidden: "Across 6 beta businesses this month, customers heard back in a median of 9
     minutes."
   - It's counted from our own records (the A-050 reply speed, pooled).
   - It appears only above a minimum (e.g. at least 3 businesses and 20 replies), and always says its base.
   - This is your call, because it's the first number on the site.

**Guardrails:**
- No fake logos, counts or quotes (A-023).
- No AI personality (S-13). Intercom names its agent "Fin"; we don't name ours.
- One primary action stays "Start free" (A-049).
