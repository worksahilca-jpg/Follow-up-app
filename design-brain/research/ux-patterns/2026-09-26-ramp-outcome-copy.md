# Ramp: features translated into short business outcomes

**Date:** 2026-09-26 · **Asked by:** Sahil · **Status:** STUDIED, proposals pending approval

**Question:** How does Ramp turn a long feature list into short outcomes a busy owner believes? Where should
FollowUp do the same?

Sources are Ramp's own pages and third-party breakdowns (listed at the end). ramp.com itself is blocked from this
environment, so page copy is quoted from search results. Nothing below is copied UI. These are principles only.

---

## What Ramp does

- **The headline is the outcome, in four words:** "Time is money. Save both." It doesn't name a single feature.
- **Every product line pairs a feature with a measured result:**
  - "close books 75% faster";
  - "save 1-2 days a week tracking expenses";
  - "intake-to-pay 3x more efficiently";
  - "full implementation in 30 days or less".
- **The outcome comes first and the feature second:** "Spend less time chasing down receipts and more time closing
  your books." It names the chore that goes away, then what you get back.
- **Big proof numbers:** "$12B and 27.5M hours saved", "5% saved on average". They're aggregated from real
  customers.
- **Four simple pillars** (cards, expenses, bills, accounting), each with one line of context. The detail lives on
  the product pages.
- A third-party critique (Coframe) says the page could state its numbers more explicitly. The numbers are the
  strongest part.

### The principles underneath

- **P1: Name the outcome, not the tool.** Say "Save both", not "an expense platform".
- **P2: One number per claim, only when it's true.** A measured result beats an adjective.
- **P3: Chore gone → thing gained.** "Less time X, more time Y" is the sentence shape.
- **P4: Short.** Each line is four to eight words. The detail is one click away.
- **P5: Aggregate proof comes from real data only.** Ramp can say 27.5M hours because it measured it.

## Where FollowUp stands

- **Hero:** "Never lose a lead because you forgot to follow up." (A-013). It's already an outcome, so it stays.
- **What changes** (A-040, PR #339): already outcomes, from the Notion study.
- **The "Features" grid still names features:**
  - Every customer in one place
  - Know who is slipping
  - Speaks their language
  - Follows up for you
  - Made for Instagram and WhatsApp
  - Works for a team
- **The pricing cards list features:**
  - "Gmail or Outlook, plus your website form"
  - "See who is slipping, and why"
  - "Bring in contacts from HubSpot or Follow Up Boss"
  - "A weekly report of what it did for you"
- **The app says what happened, but never what it saved you.** The weekly email counts customers answered, came
  back and booked (A-034/A-038), which is good. Neither the app nor the email shows time given back.

**Honesty limits (must hold):**
- No invented numbers. We have no measured "X% more bookings" and no customer totals yet (A-023).
- The numbers we can state are true by construction:
  - two minutes to connect;
  - six places customers write from;
  - the check-in schedule (days 3, 7, 14, 30);
  - it stops the moment a customer answers;
  - replies in the customer's language.

## Proposals (ranked)

1. **Rewrite the landing "Features" grid as outcome-first lines (P1, P3, P4).** The feature moves to the small line.

   | Now (feature) | Proposed (outcome) | Small line (the feature) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Every customer in one place | No message missed | Gmail, Outlook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and your website form, in one list. |
   | Know who is slipping | Catch them before they go cold | A short list of who's gone quiet, and why. |
   | Speaks their language | Answer in any language | They write in Spanish, they get Spanish back. |
   | Follows up for you | Check-ins you'd forget | Day 3, 7, 14 and 30. It stops the moment they answer. |
   | Made for Instagram and WhatsApp | Customers answer with one tap | Short messages with buttons, made for DMs. |
   | Works for a team | Nobody's customers get dropped | New customers are shared out evenly. Everyone sees what's waiting. |

2. **Rewrite the pricing cards the same way.** Keep each line under about seven words, outcome first. For example:
   - "See who is slipping, and why" → "Catch customers before they go cold"
   - "A weekly report of what it did for you" → "Every Monday: who came back, who booked"

3. **"What FollowUp did for you" in the app, in real numbers only (P2, P5).** A single line on Today and in the
   weekly email, like "This week: 12 customers answered, 2 came back, 1 booked." These counts already exist
   (weeklyDigest). An optional "about 1 hour of typing saved" figure would be an estimate (for example, 3 minutes a
   reply), so it would have to be labelled "about" and explained. **That estimate is your call.**
4. **Section headings as outcomes.** For example, "Everything you need, nothing you don't" → "Less chasing. More
   booking." (P3).
5. **Later, once testers agree (A-023):** a real proof line from real data, such as "Testers answered 214 customers
   this month." Never before that.

**Guardrails:** no invented percentages, customer counts or "X% more sales". Numbers come only from the product's
own rules or from real data.
