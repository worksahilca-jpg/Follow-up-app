# Research — UX patterns

Durable findings about **how people behave**, independent of any one product.

One file per pattern: `YYYY-MM-DD-<pattern>.md`.

## Purpose

Product references go stale — a company redesigns and your reference is obsolete. Behavior
doesn't. "People don't read; they scan for what matches their goal" was true in 1997 and
is true now. These findings are the most durable content in the design brain.

## What belongs here

- Established usability findings with sources (Nielsen Norman Group, real studies,
  large-scale product research)
- Accessibility requirements and why they exist
- Findings about the specific ICP: small-business owners, non-technical users, mobile-first
  interrupted usage
- Form, error-handling, and permission-request patterns that are known to work or fail
- Anything learned from FollowUp's own users (the most valuable category, once it exists)

## What doesn't

- "Best practices" with no evidence behind them
- Trends
- Anything you can't source

## Rules

1. **Cite the source.** This folder is where fabricated research would do the most damage,
   because a stated "study" is rarely questioned by a later session.
2. **Note the population.** A finding from enterprise software users may not transfer to a
   contractor checking a phone between jobs.
3. **Note the date.** Some findings age (mobile behavior 2010 ≠ 2026); most don't.
4. **Prefer a finding that changes a decision.** A pattern note that doesn't alter any
   FollowUp design isn't worth the file.

## Questions worth researching when they come up

Not yet answered — listed so they're not forgotten:

- How do non-technical users interpret an automated action taken on their behalf? What
  makes it feel like help vs. loss of control?
- What makes an OAuth/permission request feel safe enough to accept?
- How do people read a confidence or priority score they didn't calculate?
- What's the actual attention budget for a phone check between tasks?
- How is "this was sent automatically" best communicated in a message thread?
