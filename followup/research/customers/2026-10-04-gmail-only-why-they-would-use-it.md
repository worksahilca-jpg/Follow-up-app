# Gmail only: would an owner use FollowUp just for their inbox? — 2026-10-04

**The question (founder, 2026-10-04):** *"Suppose only Gmail works. Why would someone use FollowUp for Gmail?
Do they even face problems with leads there? Gmail has its own stuff to reply with, so why us?"* The founder
decided the same day to focus on Gmail only and recruit testers for it. This note is the evidence for and
against that focus. Grades: **A** primary source's own number, **B** a company's own page via snippet, **C** a
review site, blog or vendor study via snippet. WebFetch is blocked; nothing below was read in full.

## In short

1. **Yes, the lead is in Gmail.** Zillow and Realtor.com deliver leads by email; website forms land in the
   inbox; in our own production data 26 of 43 leads (60 percent, across 4 of 5 active accounts) came from
   Gmail. The inbox is where the lead is lost.
2. **Yes, they lose them there.** 58 percent of companies never respond to an inquiry (Drift, C). 48 percent
   of buyer inquiries to brokers got no reply at all; the average reply took 15 hours; agents made 2 email
   attempts where the best made 5 to 11 (WAV Group, 384 brokers, 2014, A for the study, old). 62 percent of
   150 small businesses gave no answer in a week (C).
3. **Gmail reminds; it does not act.** Nudges resurface a sent email after three days. Smart Reply offers
   three one-liners. Gemini drafts a reply when you ask it to. None of them sends the day-3 message, stops
   when the customer answers, tells you which emails are customers, or keeps a record. The add-on tools
   (Boomerang, Streak, Mixmax) are reminders and sales pipelines for people who already work a pipeline.
4. **Following up is worth a lot and almost nobody does it.** In the largest datasets available (cold outreach,
   so C for warm inbound), follow-ups produce 35 to 42 percent of all replies and a single follow-up adds about
   two thirds more replies; 48 percent of reps never send one. The owner's inbox is the same shape: one reply,
   then silence.
5. **So the pitch for Gmail only is honest and specific:** your Gmail already gets the leads; FollowUp answers
   them in your words within minutes, follows up on day 3, 7, 14 and 30 if they go quiet, stops the moment
   they reply, and shows you what it did. You press Send. Gmail is the mailbox; FollowUp is the person who
   works it.

## 1. Is the lead actually in Gmail?

- Zillow and Realtor.com send each inquiry as an email with the buyer's name, phone, email, the property and
  their message (B). Portal leads are email leads.
- Over 60 percent of small and mid-sized US businesses use Gmail or Google Workspace for email (C); Google
  Workspace had more than 11 million paying customers in Q4 2025 (B).
- Our own data, read-only, 2026-10-04: Gmail 26 leads across 4 businesses; website form 4; Instagram 4;
  manual 3; CSV 2; Messenger 2; phone 1; WhatsApp 1. Gmail is the majority source for every real account.

## 2. Do owners lose leads in the inbox?

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| 58 percent of companies never respond to a lead inquiry | Drift lead response report, via multiple summaries | C |
| 48 percent of buyer inquiries to 384 brokers got no response; average reply 917 minutes (15.3 h); average 2.07 email attempts; the best agents replied in 3 minutes and made 5 to 11 attempts | WAV Group Agent Responsiveness Study, 2014 | A (old) |
| Median response to a web lead across 2,241 firms: 42 hours | Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington, HBR | A |
| 62 percent of 150 small businesses gave no response within a week; 4 percent within five minutes | Finnish mystery-shopper test | C |
| 63.5 percent of 1,000 B2B SaaS companies never responded (2024 test) | independent test, via summary | C |
| Why: "on the road, inbox overflowing, important messages buried within hours" | small-business blogs | C |

The pattern is the same in every dataset: the first reply is late or never, and the second one almost never
happens. The owner is not careless; they are on a job, and the inbox has forty other things in it.

## 3. What Gmail already does, honestly

| Gmail feature | What it does | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| **Nudges** (free) | bumps a sent email with no reply back to the top after about 3 days; bumps a received email that looks unanswered | writes nothing, sends nothing, knows nothing about who is a customer |
| **Smart Reply** (free) | three one-line buttons | one line, generic, no follow-up later |
| **Gemini "Help me write"** (Workspace, paid tiers) | drafts a reply from the thread when you ask | only when you open the thread and ask; no day-3 message; no stop-on-reply; no record |
| **Schedule send, templates, labels, filters** | tools for a person who is already working the inbox | require the person |
| **Priority Inbox / categories** | sorts | does not act |

Gmail's model is: a human reads, decides and writes; Gmail helps a little at each step. FollowUp's model is:
the human decides once (Send), and the following up happens.

## 4. What the Gmail add-on tools do

| Tool | What it is | Price | Why it is not this |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boomerang | "bring this back to my inbox if nobody replies"; scheduled send; Respondable | $5 to $15 a month (B) | a reminder; the owner still writes the follow-up |
| Streak | a CRM pipeline inside Gmail | $49 to $129 per user per month (B) | a CRM; the data-entry problem in a different window |
| Mixmax | sequences, tracking, AI follow-up reminders; now a sales-engagement platform | free to $89 a month (B) | built for outbound salespeople running sequences, not an owner answering inbound |
| Gmelius | shared inbox for teams | about $15 per user (C) | team collaboration |
| Mailbutler, Yesware, FollowUpThen | tracking, reminders, snooze | $5 to $30 | reminders |

Every one of them either reminds the human or gives the human a bigger tool. None reads the inbox, picks
out the customers, writes the reply in the owner's voice, holds it for one tap, follows up on a schedule and
stops on reply. That combination, on Gmail alone, is still unoccupied at the solo-owner price.

## 5. Is following up worth it?

- Follow-ups produce 42 percent of all replies in a 20-million-email dataset; steps 2 to 4 alone produce
  35 percent (Instantly, Woodpecker; C, cold outreach).
- A single follow-up produces 65.8 percent more replies than the first email alone (Backlinko, 12 million
  outreach emails; C, cold outreach).
- The first follow-up gets about 40 percent higher reply rate than the initial message (Woodpecker; C).
- 48 percent of reps never send a follow-up (C). WAV's brokers averaged 2 attempts against a best practice
  of 5 to 11 (A, old).
- 78 percent of customers buy from the company that responds first (Drift via summaries; C).

These are outbound numbers; warm inbound replies better, so the direction is safe even if the size is not.
The owner's own numbers are what will matter, which is why the proof line on Today ("3 answered after a
check-in") is the next thing to build.

## 6. Why someone would use FollowUp for Gmail only (the answer)

**The problem they feel:** "I get leads by email and I answer them late or forget, and I never chase the ones
who went quiet." They know this. WAV, Drift and the 42-hour median say most of them do it every week.

**What Gmail gives them:** a nudge three days later and a draft if they ask. Still their job.

**What FollowUp gives them, today, in the code:** the customer emails are picked out of the inbox; a reply is
written in their words from their own past replies; it waits for one tap (or sends itself if they choose);
day 3, 7, 14 and 30 check-ins if the customer goes quiet; it stops the moment the customer answers; a record
of what it did and why it held anything. Nothing to set up beyond "Connect Gmail".

**In one sentence:** *Your Gmail already gets the leads. FollowUp answers them in your words within minutes,
follows up if they go quiet, and stops the moment they reply. You just press Send.*

**What we must not claim:** "a trained model that closes deals". It is not that, and the owner will hear it as
spam-tool talk (`CLAUDE.md`, trust). It writes like them from their replies and follows up until the customer
answers. That is enough, and it is true.

## 7. What "Gmail only" should change, and what it should not

- **Change:** who we recruit (owners whose leads arrive by email), what the landing page leads with, what
  onboarding asks first, which proof number we build (replies within the hour, customers who answered after a
  check-in), and where the next ten testers come from.
- **Do not change yet:** the code for the other channels. Instagram and Messenger reviews are in flight and
  cost nothing to leave; the website form is one line of HTML. They stay available, not promoted.
- **Risk to watch:** Google's own direction. Gemini in Gmail gets stronger every quarter. Gmail could ship
  "follow up for me" one day. The defence is the same as before: act, not remind; the owner's voice; the
  record; and the multi-channel view the day it is needed.
