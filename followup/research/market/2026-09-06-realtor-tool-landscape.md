## Part 1 — Named tool findings (adoption, pricing, who it's for, real sentiment)

### Follow Up Boss (already covered for features/pricing in the 09-05 pass — adoption data only, here)
Already the most-cited standing incumbent in the segment. Search-snippet evidence: **41 of the top 50 US real estate teams use Follow Up Boss**, it processes **25M+ leads/year across 100K+ agents** with a claimed 98.2% customer-satisfaction figure, and it's now owned by **Zillow Group** (acquired late 2023) — i.e., the market leader isn't an independent startup, it's a line item inside the biggest real estate portal in the US. On Reddit specifically, "Follow Up Boss wins the organic praise contest by a mile" was the clearest unprompted signal found — one agent's post about jumping from 12% to 31% lead-to-appointment conversion after switching was the single most-repeated anecdote across summarized threads.
*Sources: search snippets summarizing followupboss.com/pricing, followupboss.com/customer-stories, tipranks.com (Zillow acquisition), and Reddit r/realtors discussion as indexed — accessed via WebSearch 2026-09-06, not fetched directly.*
**Read:** FUB is the realtor-CRM category's Schelling point. Any new entrant is competing against a tool with a Zillow-scale distribution channel behind it, not a scrappy startup.

### kvCORE / BoldTrail (Inside Real Estate)
**Current name:** kvCORE was rebranded to **BoldTrail** in 2024; "kvCORE" still gets used interchangeably in agent conversation and search volume, so both names matter for positioning purposes. **Pricing:** no longer published — sales-gated quotes, roughly **$499–750/mo for a solo agent**, **$500–1,500/mo for teams**, **$1,500+/mo for large brokerages** (50+ agent brokerages can negotiate under $25/agent/mo). **Who it's for:** brokerage-level, top-down deployments — it's typically the brokerage that buys it and hands it to agents, not an individual agent shopping for a CRM. **Real sentiment:** this is where the research got genuinely useful — multiple review aggregators independently describe **aggressive sales tactics, 12-month auto-renewing contracts with 30–90 day cancellation windows, no published pricing, no free trial, and slow support** ("leave a voicemail and hope for a callback in a few days"). One quoted Reddit-style comment: *"I've been using BoldTrail for about six months now, and I can honestly say it has been the most frustrating software experience I've had in real estate."* Common complaint pattern: fine if you're a big brokerage with a dedicated CRM admin, actively resented by small teams stuck with it because their brokerage mandated it.
*Sources: luxurypresence.com/blogs/kvcore-pricing, theprotoolkit.com/kvcore-pricing-2026, adamationai.com/blog/boldtrail-reviews-small-brokerage-2026 — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*
**Read:** kvCORE/BoldTrail is entrenched by brokerage-level contracts, not agent love — its moat is organizational inertia (a brokerage-wide switch is a political decision, not a $29/mo impulse buy), not product satisfaction. That's a genuinely different kind of incumbency than Follow Up Boss's — harder to displace with a better product, easier to pick off one frustrated agent at a time.

### Lofty (formerly Chime)
Rebranded from Chime in 2023; **owned by Moatable, Inc.** (formerly Renren). **Pricing:** $449–1,500/mo plus a $299 setup fee, tiered Agent/Team/Broker/Enterprise, not publicly itemized. **Scale claim:** "serves over 70,000 real estate professionals" per its own marketing (vendor-stated, uncorroborated by an independent count). **Positioning:** an all-in-one bundle — IDX website, CRM, paid lead-gen, and **its own "AI follow-up tooling."** That last point matters directly for FollowUp's pitch: Lofty already markets AI-driven follow-up as a bundled feature, not a gap.
*Sources: luxurypresence.com/blogs/lofty-review, realsavvy.com/lofty-pricing, aiforproptech.com/companies/lofty — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*

### LionDesk — **officially dead, not just declining**
This is the most decision-relevant single fact in this pass. LionDesk was acquired by Lone Wolf Technologies in 2021 (bundled with HomeSpotter); Lone Wolf announced in **May 2025** it would wind the product down, kept it live only through **September 2025** for data-migration purposes, and as of "early 2026" only offers a limited legacy export portal — former customers are being funneled to **Lone Wolf Relationships**, a new CRM under Lone Wolf's "Foundation" enterprise initiative. LionDesk was historically one of the cheapest, most name-recognized budget options for solo agents (frequently namechecked alongside Follow Up Boss and kvCORE in older forum threads) — it no longer exists as a product a realtor can sign up for today.
*Sources: inman.com/2025/05/16 (Lone Wolf winding down LionDesk), housingwire.com (Lone Wolf acquires LionDesk and HomeSpotter), clientaro.com/blog/best-liondesk-alternatives-2026 — via WebSearch 2026-09-06, cross-checked across three independent domains, consistent story on all three.*
**Read:** this is a real, concrete churn event — a named cohort of price-sensitive solo/small-team agents just lost their CRM and is actively being asked to migrate somewhere. It's a legitimate wedge moment, but it's also an old-news event by the time most agents act on it (the sunset was Sept 2025, a year before this write-up), and every "LionDesk alternatives 2026" listicle already exists — FollowUp isn't the only one who noticed.

### Wise Agent
**Pricing:** $49/mo (shared login, up to 5 users) or $326/yr; realistic solo cost ~$60/mo once texting/individual logins are added, ~$140/mo for a 5-person team. **Who it's for:** explicitly the budget/solo-agent tier — the cheapest name that keeps coming up as "what agents recommend for someone who can't/won't pay Follow Up Boss money." **Sentiment:** consistently praised for support quality (24/7, personal, responsive) and value; 4.4/5 aggregate rating; complaints center on a "crowded" UI and features that are harder to learn than the low price suggests.
*Sources: capterra.com/p/20680 (Wise Agent), selecthub.com, luxurypresence.com/blogs/wise-agent-review — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*
**Read:** Wise Agent is the closest existing analog to FollowUp's low-flat-price positioning inside the realtor segment specifically — at $49–60/mo it's already roughly 2x FollowUp's $29/mo but clearly occupies the "I just want something cheap and simple" slot FollowUp would want to compete for. It is not a full-autonomy AI product; it's a traditional CRM at a friendly price.

### BoomTown
**Pricing:** $1,000–1,700/mo quoted, frequently rising to $2,500+/mo once ad-management fees (15% of ad spend), setup fees, and MLS pass-throughs are included. **Who it's for:** large, established teams/brokerages (20+ agents) with a real PPC ad budget ($5k+/mo) — explicitly not a solo-agent tool. **Sentiment:** support praised; the most common complaint is **lead quality** — "low-quality or fake leads," leads that "rarely convert into actual business" — i.e., the paid-lead-gen half of the bundle draws more agent frustration than the CRM half.
*Sources: luxurypresence.com/blogs/boomtown-pricing, dmrmedia.org/blog/boomtown-reviews, consumeraffairs.com/homeowners/boomtown — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*

### Real Geeks
**Pricing:** ~$249–299/mo for up to 2 users plus a $250 onboarding fee; higher tiers driven by its "Real Leads" PPC add-on. **Who it's for:** small teams wanting an affordable IDX-website + CRM + lead-gen bundle — consistently positioned by reviewers as the budget alternative to BoomTown/CINC/Sierra Interactive, not to Follow Up Boss or Wise Agent (different comparison set — bundled lead-gen platforms, not standalone CRMs). **Sentiment:** praised for ease of use, support, and price-to-feature ratio; dinged for a dated interface.
*Sources: theclose.com/real-geeks-review, agentadvice.com/real-geeks-review, capterra.com/p/222439 — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*

### Realvolve
**Pricing:** Essential $59/user/mo, Professional $99/user/mo (some listings show a $94/mo aggregate figure — treat the per-user numbers as primary). **Positioning:** workflow-automation-first — its differentiator is genuinely configurable multi-step, conditional-branching workflows (not just canned drip campaigns), which shows up as a double-edged reputation: 4.3/5 on Capterra but only 3.6/5 on G2, and the recurring theme is "great for teams willing to invest in setup, a steep, demanding learning curve for solo agents and less-technical users."
*Sources: selecthub.com/realvolve, getapp.com/real-estate-property-software/a/realvolve, itqlick.com/realvolve-crm — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*

### CINC and Top Producer (found via forum/comparison research, not in the original list — adding per instructions)
**CINC:** $899–1,299/mo for a solo agent, scaling with lead volume/seats — it does not sell CRM software without bundled done-for-you lead generation, so it's structurally a lead-gen product with a CRM attached, aimed at high-volume teams, not a CRM a price-sensitive solo agent would ever consider. **Top Producer:** $129/mo base, $179/user/mo Pro tier — positioned as a more affordable standalone CRM with direct MLS integration (320+ boards) as its headline differentiator (property alerts/just-listed notifications sourced straight from MLS). Both come up constantly in "best real estate CRM 2026" listicles and forum comparison threads alongside Follow Up Boss and kvCORE — genuine mid-market incumbents, just not on the original research list.
*Sources: softwareadvice.com/compare/34818-top-producer/vs/71237-cinc, sierrainteractive.com/insights/blog/best-real-estate-crm, jamilacademy.com/blog/real-estate-crm-and-platform-pricing — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*

### The two tools that matter most for FollowUp's specific thesis: Ylopo and Structurely
These weren't on the original research list but turned out to be the single most important finding in this pass, because they are **already doing, specifically for real estate, exactly what FollowUp's mission statement claims nobody is doing.**

- **Ylopo** — "Database Ignite" feature uses AI to automatically identify and re-engage **cold contacts already sitting in an agent's existing database**, via AI voice + text, claiming up to 5x higher response rates and re-engagement costs as low as $0.90/lead. This is not a hypothetical roadmap item — it's a shipping, marketed feature, positioned explicitly around "your dead leads are worth reviving."
- **Structurely** — its AI inside-sales-agent product, branded **"Aisa Holmes,"** engages new leads across **SMS, email, web chat, and voice**, and — critically — **nurtures them for 12+ months** until sales-ready, i.e., it is explicitly built to carry a lead through the exact "interstellar / never reached / gone cold" phase the CEO's mission statement names as the gap nobody is filling. It has **13M+ conversations processed** and a claimed 57% response rate. And it is **already bilingual in English and Spanish** (shipped, not roadmap) — explicitly marketed against the US's 55M Spanish-speaking population, with broader multilingual voice claimed in adjacent "best AI tools for real estate" roundups. Pricing: $179/mo (50 leads) up to $499/mo (250 leads), often plus a ~$2,000 onboarding fee — aimed at teams with real lead volume, not solo agents.
*Sources: ylopo.com/real-estate-lead-nurture, superdupr.com/blog/structurely-vs-ylopo-vs-roof-ai, structurely.com/blog/structurely-spanish, retellai.com/blog/best-ai-tools-real-estate-agents, hooquest.com/isa/structurely — via WebSearch 2026-09-06, Structurely's Spanish-language claim cross-checked against a second independent source (Zendesk's own write-up of Structurely) and consistent.*

There is also a broader, fast-growing sub-category of **AI voice-agent platforms explicitly marketed at real estate** (CloudTalk's "CeTe," Aloware, EchoLeads, LuMay, and others found in 2026-dated "best AI voice agent for real estate" roundups) — these are frequently framed around exactly FollowUp's pitch: answer every call, qualify, respond in under 60 seconds, operate 24/7, and — repeatedly and explicitly — **support 50+ languages with automatic mid-call language detection**. This is not a niche or emerging claim; it's the standard marketing copy for this entire sub-category as of 2026.
*Source: cloudtalk.io/blog/best-ai-voice-agents-for-real-estate, ainora.lt/blog/ai-voice-agent-real-estate-agencies-2026, lumay.ai/blogs/best-ai-voice-agent-platforms-for-real-estate — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*

## Part 2 — Category-level findings

1. **The realtor CRM/lead-follow-up market is not a gap waiting to be found — it's one of the most densely served SaaS categories in existence.** Between the eight core tools requested plus CINC and Top Producer, this pass found ten established, currently-active, well-reviewed products, several backed by companies with real capital (Zillow owns Follow Up Boss; Moatable owns Lofty; Lone Wolf — itself a large real-estate-infrastructure roll-up — owns what used to be LionDesk). Every "best real estate CRM 2026" listicle compares the same 10-15 names. A new entrant isn't discovering an empty category; it's the eleventh or twelfth name on an already-long list agents are shown constantly.

2. **NAR's own 2025 Technology Survey (1,241 responses, July 2025) confirms CRM adoption is already mainstream, not a frontier.** eSignature (79%) and social media (75%) lead tool categories; CRM systems are listed among the "most members regularly use" tools; **21% of agents already report using a CRM with AI-powered insights**, and AI tool usage overall is at 68% (mostly ChatGPT, at 58% of AI users). A third of agents already spend $50–250/mo on technology, a quarter spend $500+/mo. This is a market that has already adopted CRM and is actively adopting AI-in-CRM — the "nobody is doing this" framing doesn't hold for real estate specifically the way it might for an unserved SMB category.
*Source: nar.realtor/press-releases/realtors-embrace-ai-digital-tools-to-enhance-client-service-nar-survey-finds, housingwire.com/articles/nar-2025-technology-survey — via WebSearch 2026-09-06.*

3. **The "cold/unreached lead" pain point is real and widely acknowledged in the realtor community — but it's already the explicit marketed hook of at least two funded, real-estate-specific competitors (Ylopo, Structurely), not an unaddressed niche.** Stats repeatedly cited in agent-facing content: 44% of agents stop following up after one "no," 87% of agents still lose deals to poor follow-up, 93% of past-client/sphere contacts eventually transact with a *different* agent due to inconsistent nurture. The pain is genuine and well-documented — but the market's response to it (AI-driven database reactivation, 12-month AI nurture sequences, multilingual AI voice/text) is also genuine, shipped, and specifically built for realtors, not generic.

4. **Pricing pattern holds, with one important twist.** Every traditional CRM in this list (kvCORE $499+, Lofty $449+, BoomTown $1,000+, CINC $899+, Realvolve $59-99/user, Real Geeks $249+, Top Producer $129+, Wise Agent $49+) is priced well above FollowUp's flat $29/mo — consistent with the 09-05 pass's finding on Follow Up Boss/Close/Podium. **But the AI-nurture specialists (Structurely $179-499/mo, Ylopo's add-on pricing) are priced in the same expensive tier**, meaning FollowUp's flat-price advantage would still be real *if* it entered real estate — it just wouldn't be pairing a price advantage with a feature/positioning advantage the way it currently can against generic horizontal CRMs. The realtor-specific AI-nurture competitors have both the feature *and* usually the higher price, so FollowUp would be underselling functionally-similar tools rather than delivering something categorically new.

5. **LionDesk's 2025 shutdown is the one live, concrete opportunity in this pass, though it's not exclusive to FollowUp.** A named cohort of price-sensitive agents already had to migrate off a budget CRM within the last year. But every "LionDesk alternatives" listicle (Wise Agent, Follow Up Boss, kvCORE, etc.) already routes that traffic to the existing incumbents above — FollowUp would be one option among many being pointed at the same displaced users, not the only one positioned to catch them.

## Part 3 — The honest answer to the actual question

**Is there a real, current gap in what realtors are actually using that FollowUp's "rescue cold/unreached leads, full autonomy eventually, every language" angle could exploit?**

**No — not distinctively, and this needs to be said plainly rather than talked around.** Every piece of the pitch that reads as differentiated in FollowUp's general-market positioning already has a shipped, real-estate-specific, currently-marketed answer in this category:

- "Rescue cold/never-reached leads" → **Ylopo's Database Ignite** and **Structurely's Aisa Holmes** (12+ month AI nurture) already do exactly this, by name, as their headline pitch, for real estate specifically.
- "Full autonomy, AI doing the follow-up job instead of a human" → Structurely's whole positioning is "AI ISA replaces a human inside-sales-agent"; the broader AI-voice-agent sub-category (CloudTalk, Aloware, EchoLeads, LuMay, etc.) markets the same "AI answers so no human has to" promise, specifically to realtors, as boilerplate copy in 2026.
- "Every language, not just English" → Structurely already ships bilingual English/Spanish AI nurture; the AI-voice-agent sub-category's standard marketing claim is 50+ languages with automatic mid-call detection. This is not a future differentiator in real estate — it's already table stakes among the AI-native competitors in this specific vertical.

What FollowUp *would* still have, if it went into real estate anyway, is **price** (flat $29/mo against a field where even the AI-nurture specialists charge $179-1,500+/mo) and possibly a **simpler, single-product story** (one flat-price tool doing capture + scoring + follow-up, vs. stitching a CRM to a separate $200+/mo AI-ISA add-on). Those are real, legitimate angles — but they're a *pricing/packaging* wedge into an already-crowded, well-funded, actively-innovating vertical, not evidence of an unmet need nobody else has spotted. The realtor segment does not look like "nobody is doing this" — it looks like "several venture-backed, real-estate-only companies are already doing this and have been for a few years, and the generalist incumbents (Zillow-owned Follow Up Boss, Lofty/Moatable, kvCORE/Inside Real Estate) are large enough to acquire or clone whatever gains traction," which is a materially different, harder starting position than the home-services-contractor data point already on file (`research/customers/2026-09-05-icp-pain-and-trust-objections.md`), where no comparably-specific AI-nurture competitor turned up in that research.

**Flag for the CEO/manager-agent:** if real estate stays on the table, the honest pitch is "cheapest full-stack option in an already-AI-native vertical," not "we're the first to solve the cold-lead problem for realtors" — the second framing would not survive five minutes of due diligence against Structurely's or Ylopo's own marketing pages. This finding doesn't kill real estate as a vertical outright (price/packaging wedges are legitimate strategies), but it does mean real estate is a *harder*, more contested vertical bet than the home-services data point currently on file, and any go/no-go decision should weigh that contest explicitly rather than assume a clear field.
