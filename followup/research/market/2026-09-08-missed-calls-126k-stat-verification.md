# Verifying the "$126,000/year lost to missed calls" stat

Checked: 2026-09-08. Fact-checking the highest-stakes stat in
`research/market/2026-09-07-why-followup-evidence-for-and-against.md` (currently graded C,
vendor-published): "Estimated annual revenue lost to missed calls per small business: ~$126,000."
WebFetch blocked; WebSearch only.

## Verdict: the number is real and widely repeated, but stays graded C — no traceable primary
## methodology found, and the trail is shorter/thinner than the repetition suggests

**Attribution found**: multiple secondary sources (Phone2, GetAira, dialora.ai, and others, all
2026 marketing-content blogs) attribute the $126,000 figure to **"Invoca and BIA/Kelsey"** research.
This is a named, real pair of organizations — Invoca is a real call-tracking/analytics vendor,
BIA/Kelsey a real market-research firm that covers local-business advertising — so this is not a
fabricated statistic. But **no source found actually links to or quotes an Invoca/BIA-Kelsey report
by name, date, or URL** — every citation found is one blog citing another blog's characterization
of "research from Invoca and BIA/Kelsey," which is exactly the "vendor-published, self-interested"
shape the existing C grade already anticipates. **Grade stays C** — this session did not surface a
document that would justify upgrading it to B (a secondary source that actually names and links a
specific primary report) or A.

**Likely underlying arithmetic, inferred (not confirmed) this session**: the same source cluster
also cites "**each missed call costs approximately $1,200 in lost revenue**" — and one source
traces *that* narrower figure back to "a 2021 industry benchmarks analysis" (also not independently
located or named). $126,000 / $1,200 ≈ 105 missed calls/year, or roughly 2/week — a plausible
order of magnitude for a small local-service business, but this is this researcher's arithmetic
inference from two independently-repeated numbers, not something any source stated as the actual
calculation. **Nothing found discloses the real methodology** — sample size, industries covered,
survey vs. call-tracking-log-derived, or even a publication year for the $126,000 figure
specifically (the $1,200/call component traces to ~2021; whether $126,000 is contemporaneous with
that or a later, possibly inflation-adjusted or re-derived figure is unknown).

**Corroborating context that's on firmer ground**: the *inputs* around this stat are better
sourced than the headline number itself. The existing doc's 62% missed-call rate (411 Locals, 85
businesses/58 industries, graded B) and the 85%-never-call-back figure both independently recur
across this session's searches too, and Invoca's own more specific home-services figures (27%
miss rate, ~$1,200/missed call for that vertical specifically) were repeated consistently enough
across sources to be closer to credible-secondary than the all-industries $126,000 headline
number, which reads as a rounder, more marketing-friendly aggregate.

## Recommendation

Keep the $126,000 figure in the corpus, keep it graded C, and add one line making the trail
explicit: attributed across secondary sources to "Invoca and BIA/Kelsey" but no source located
this session actually names/links the underlying report — treat it as directionally credible
(the components — high miss rates, real per-call value, no-callback behavior — are independently
better sourced) rather than a number to cite with precision (e.g., don't say "$126,000" in
anything customer- or investor-facing without a firmer citation; "small businesses can lose over
$100K/year" is about as far as this evidence actually supports).
