# Realtor AI competitor refresh: Structurely and Ylopo, what's changed since 2026-09-06

**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Scope:** Tight follow-up to `research/market/2026-09-06-realtor-tool-landscape.md` — checking
only whether anything material has shifted for Structurely and Ylopo since that pass, to inform
whether Rule 1 (stay horizontal, real estate declined as a vertical) still holds. Not re-deriving
anything already in the prior doc. WebFetch still blocked; WebSearch-snippet-sourced throughout.

## What changed

**1. Structurely was acquired by CapStone Holdings Inc. on 2026-01-06 — this is new and
decision-relevant.** Confirmed across the acquirer's own PR Newswire release, Structurely's own
news page, and a secondary pickup (pulse2.com), plus CapStone's own SEC 10-Q filings referencing
the deal — this is about as well-corroborated as a WebSearch-snippet pass gets without a direct
fetch. *Graded B — a company's own SEC filing (10-Q/NT 10-Q) is close to primary, cross-checked
against the press release and an independent pickup; still not fetched directly.* Framing from the
acquirer: "positioning the company for the next era of AI-driven sales automation," explicitly
citing Structurely's 9-year track record — 13M+ AI-driven conversations, 75M+ AI-generated
messages, 5.5M+ human-labeled transcripts used for model training — as the asset being bought.
**Read: Structurely is no longer an independent startup; it's now backed by a public holding
company with stated intent to scale it further, not sunset or fold it in quietly.** That's a
capital-backing upgrade for FollowUp's closest real-estate-specific analog, not a weakening.

**2. Structurely's pricing has moved up.** The 2026-09-06 doc had it at $179/mo (50 leads) to
$499/mo (250 leads) plus a ~$2,000 onboarding fee. Current search snippets (Capterra, G2) show
published plans **now starting at $299/mo**, post-acquisition. *Graded C — aggregator/listing
sites, not Structurely's own pricing page content, via WebSearch 2026-09-08.* Read together with
finding 1: entry price rose roughly 65% since the acquisition, consistent with a company being
repositioned upmarket rather than staying a scrappy budget option.

**3. Ylopo shipped a materially bigger AI product since the last pass — "AI2 (Squared)" / Raiya,
launched around September 2025.** The 2026-09-06 doc only had Ylopo's "Database Ignite" cold-lead
reactivation feature (AI voice+text, ~$0.90/lead). This pass found Ylopo has since branded and
expanded a full AI assistant, **Raiya** — described as "behavioral, not scripted," monitoring
each lead's on-site activity and handling the first 10-15 touches of a new lead automatically
before handing off to the agent. *Graded C, via ylopo.com's own FAQ/pricing pages and
proptoolfinder.com, ylopo.com/is-ylopo-worth-it, WebSearch 2026-09-08 — no independent
funding-round or press confirmation found for this specific launch beyond Ylopo's own site, so
treat the "AI2/Squared" branding and exact Sept 2025 date as vendor-stated, not independently
dated.*

**4. Raiya is priced well above the old Database Ignite figure — a real product/pricing escalation, not just a rename.** Search snippets converge on **Raiya starting around $500-795/mo** as an
add-on, on top of Ylopo's base platform fee (~$300-600/mo) and a Ylopo-recommended **minimum
$500-1,000/mo ad spend** — all-in cost estimates for a working Ylopo setup now run **$1,500-3,500+/mo**. *Graded C, multiple aggregator sites (luxurypresence.com, bounti.ai) converging on
overlapping ranges, via WebSearch 2026-09-08 — Ylopo does not publish exact numbers (deliberately,
per its own "See Your ROI, Then Your Price" pricing-page framing), so treat these as directional
estimates only.*

**No funding-round news found for either company** beyond the Structurely acquisition itself —
searched directly and found nothing for Ylopo beyond a stale 2017 debt-financing record surfaced
by Crunchbase's own data (clearly outdated, not a 2026 event, noted so it isn't mistaken for
current).

## Does this change the calculus on Rule 1 (stay horizontal)?

**No — if anything it reinforces the 2026-09-06 decision rather than reopening it.** The prior
doc's conclusion was that real estate is contested by funded, AI-native competitors already
selling almost exactly FollowUp's pitch (cold-lead rescue, multilingual, full autonomy) to that
exact segment. Both changes found here point the same direction, harder:
- Structurely getting acquired by a capital-backed holding company (not funded by a VC round, but
  functionally similar — a company committing capital specifically to scale this product) is the
  opposite of a competitor weakening or exiting.
- Ylopo materially expanding its AI product (Database Ignite → a full branded assistant handling
  first-touch conversation) in the ~10 months since the last research pass shows the vertical is
  actively getting more crowded on the AI-native-feature axis, not settling.
- Both companies' prices moved **up**, not down, since the last pass — Structurely's entry price
  rose from $179 to $299/mo; Ylopo's AI add-on alone (Raiya, $500-795/mo) now costs more than
  Structurely's entire old starting tier. This widens, not narrows, FollowUp's price-advantage
  angle if it ever entered real estate (still the one legitimate wedge the 09-06 doc identified),
  but it doesn't change the underlying finding that the *feature* territory (AI cold-lead rescue,
  autonomous nurture) is already claimed and getting more capitalized, not less.

**Flag for CEO/manager-agent:** nothing here warrants reopening Rule 1. This is a confirmation
pass, not a new signal — file it as evidence the 2026-09-06 decision continues to hold, and revisit
only if a future pass finds an actual funding round, a shutdown, or a pricing *drop* for either
company (none of which appeared here).
