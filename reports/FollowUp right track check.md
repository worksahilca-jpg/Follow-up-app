# FollowUp must trade polish for realtors

**In short**

1. **Verdict: partly on track.** You picked the right problem, but your time is going to the wrong work.
2. Almost nobody uses it yet. In the last 30 days: 1 customer answered through FollowUp, 0 bookings, 1 new signup.
3. It is off right now. AI credits ran out on Oct 30. Turn it back on today.
4. The market is real (Yelp paid about $270M for a similar tool), but big players now give the first reply away.
5. Your best shot: Toronto-area solo realtors who live in Gmail and Instagram and want follow-up after the first reply, sent only with their OK.
6. Next 30 days: set up your 9 signed-up businesses yourself on a call, talk to 10–15 new realtors a week, and charge $39 from day one with a money-back promise.
7. Count one number every week: realtors with at least one customer answered through FollowUp.
8. Stop new features, redesigns, admin tools and big AI test runs unless a real realtor is stuck.
9. Rules to plan around: no automatic Instagram or Messenger messages after 24 hours, a name, address and unsubscribe in every email (Canada's spam law), and the brokerage named.
10. Decide on Nov 30. If 5 realtors are active in one week and 3 are paying, keep going. If fewer than 3 are active after 40+ conversations, change who it's for.

**How to read the tags.** *(strong)* means an official or first-hand source, or many sources that agree. *(medium)* means trusted press, or an expert reported second-hand. *(weak)* means one blog, a vendor or a summary. *(our data)* means FollowUp's own numbers. *(my estimate)* means a target or judgment made in this report, not a published figure. This is research, not legal advice.

## Partly on track: the idea holds, but six weeks went to the wrong work

FollowUp works in tests but is barely used by real businesses, and use is falling. These are your numbers as of Nov 1, with founder accounts left out *(our data)*:

| | Since September | Last 30 days | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outside businesses signed up | 9 | 1 | New signups have almost stopped |
| Customers captured | 25 | 5, across 3 businesses | About 1.7 per active business a month |
| Customers answered through FollowUp | 8 | 1 | The main job barely happened |
| Bookings | 0 | 0 | No proof yet to sell with |
| Paying | 1 marked paying, not checked in Stripe | — | Treat as 0 or 1 until Stripe confirms it |
| AI working | No AI call has worked since Oct 30 | — | The product cannot reply today |

**By every early-stage yardstick, this is "pushing", not "pulling."** Paul Graham's test is weekly growth: 5–7% a week is good, and 1% means "you haven't yet figured out what you're doing" *(strong)* ([summary of PG's essay](https://kamilski81.blogspot.com/2014/05/startup-growth-by-paul-graham-summary.html)). FollowUp's weekly growth in active businesses is about zero. Marc Andreessen's signs that it isn't working yet include use that isn't growing and no word of mouth *(strong; wording paraphrased)* ([Bokardo](https://bokardo.com/archives/origin-product-market-fit/)). First Round's lowest level of real fit is about **3–5 customers who are very happy** *(medium)* ([One Cerebral](https://onecerebral.beehiiv.com/p/this-vc-s-framework-to-finding-product-market-fit)). FollowUp is below that: 8 of 9 businesses had no customer answered through FollowUp in 30 days. One vendor puts the average share of signups who reach their first real value at **37.5%**, and 42.6% for CRM tools *(weak, vendor data)* ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/activation-metrics-saas/)). FollowUp is at about 11% (1 of 9).

**The stage itself is normal.** Jobber's founders visited businesses daily for more than six months to find their first customer *(medium)* ([Agents of Scale transcript](https://agentsofscale.transistor.fm/6/transcript)), and had about three customers paying $29 a month after a year *(weak)* ([Network Effects](https://network-effects.beehiiv.com/p/jobber-the-operating-system-for-local-service-pros)). Follow Up Boss's early growth is described as "glacial" *(weak)* ([podcast summary](https://podpulse.ai/podcast/my-first-million/my-first-million-24m-in-one-day)). Lenny Rachitsky finds B2B companies normally take about two years to start feeling real fit *(medium)* ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/finding-product-market-fit)). Being at roughly one paying business two months after the first outside signups is not the problem.

**What is off track is where the hours went.** Most of the last six weeks went to polish, AI accuracy tests and admin tools while signups fell from 8 (September to early October) to 1. Your own goal for Oct 31 was 5 testers using it every day and 20+ customers answered a week; the real figure was 1 in 30 days *(our data)*. On Oct 30 the AI tests spent about **$41 in one day, while real customers used under $1 in seven days**, and the tests are what used up the credits *(our data, project log)*. The Oct 29 internal review already said "stop adding features and get 3 realtors using it daily"; the days after went to test tooling *(our data)*. This matches the best-known failure pattern. In CB Insights' study of 101 failed startups, "no market need" was the top reason, at 42% *(strong as a dataset, but self-reported)* ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/kimberly-weisul/what-went-wrong-failed-startups-tell-all.html)). The Startup Genome study found that startups which grew too early wrote about 3.4x more code while still finding out what customers wanted *(weak to medium, secondary summary)* ([Startupgeist](https://medium.com/startupgeist-articles/startup-failure-why-startups-fail-e6c4856b65a7)). AI coding tools make this trap easier: "vibe coding just made it easier to skip that part," as one mentor puts it *(weak, opinion)* ([MentorCruise](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/the-vibe-coding-trap-why-founders-are-building-faster-and-understanding-less-77f0c/)).

**The low numbers may be a setup problem, not a demand problem.** An earlier internal note estimates a solo realtor gets 10–50 inquiries a month *(weak)*, yet FollowUp saw about 1.7 per active business. On Oct 30, 3 of 5 Gmail connections were not connected, two of them cut off by Google's old 7-day test limit. On Oct 29, 40 of 56 unsent drafts were more than 30 days old, meaning owners were not opening the app *(our data; both counts include test accounts)*. The plainest reading: FollowUp is not wired into where realtors' customers arrive, and owners are not opening it. You can fix both by hand. It also means you **cannot yet tell "nobody wants this" from "not enough selling and setup."** YC's Dalton Caldwell draws exactly this line before any change of direction *(medium)* ([YC Startup School summary](https://summify.io/discover/yc-sus-eric-migicovsky-dalton-caldwell-pivoting-pitching-msWFar)). So don't change direction now.

## The market pays, but the first reply is now nearly free

**People pay real money for this.** Yelp agreed to buy Hatch, an AI tool that answers and follows up with new customers, for **about $270M in cash plus $30M in retention payments**. Hatch had about $25M in yearly revenue, growing 70% a year *(strong)* ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260121295483/en/Yelp-Accelerates-Strategy-with-Acquisition-of-AI-Lead-Management-Platform-Hatch)). Zillow folded AI follow-up into Follow Up Boss as "Zillow Pro," reported at $138 a month for one agent *(strong for the launch, medium for the price)* ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/10/15/zillow-unveils-new-ai-powered-technology-suite-zillow-pro/)). eXp Realty chose Cloze, an inbox-first AI CRM, for its agents *(strong)* ([Nasdaq release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/exp-realty-selects-clozes-ai-powered-real-estate-platform-crm-choice-program-2025-07)). Canada has **more than 160,000 REALTORS®** *(strong)* ([CREA](https://www.crea.ca/media-hub/news/the-canadian-real-estate-association-installs-2025-2026-board-of-directors/)). In the US, 48% of agents now use AI daily or weekly *(strong)* ([Inman on NAR 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/09/22/nar-technology-report-ai-adoption/)), but in 2025 only 7% used chatbots to capture customers *(strong)* ([HousingWire on NAR 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2025-technology-survey-realtor-tech-usage-trends/)).

**It is also crowded and getting cheap.** An instant first reply now costs about $15 a month with Instagram DM tools *(medium)* ([SetSmart](https://setsmart.io/blog/real-estate-chatbot)) and $30 a month from Yelp *(medium)* ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/yelp-charges-30-a-month-for-ai-agent-that-answers-and-qualifies-leads/)). Meta launched its own Business Agent free, though reports on its later pricing conflict *(weak)* ([Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/meta-business-agent-billing-august-1-token-pricing-2026/)). Most realtor CRMs now include or sell an AI layer. "AI that replies to your customers" is no longer a reason to pay.

**Budgets fit $39, not much more.** 36% of US agents spend $50–$250 a month on all their tech and 18% spend under $50. The two biggest barriers to new tools are the learning curve (63%) and cost (59%) *(strong)* ([The Close](https://theclose.com/stats-trends/news-real-estate-tech-spending/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/realtors-ai-use-2026/)). **Only 46% use a CRM at all** *(strong)* ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/09/22/nar-technology-report-ai-adoption/)). $39 fits that budget; $79 is a hard sell to a solo agent unless it replaces something they already pay for *(my estimate)*. Price is not the problem today, because nobody has seen enough value yet to test it.

**One caution: solo realtors are not proven easy first buyers.** Structurely, the closest AI follow-up tool for realtors, says it suits teams rather than individual agents *(medium)* ([case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/structurely/case-study/the-deberry-team-of-keller-williams-customer-case-study)). Acrely, a 2025 YC company, started with real estate agents, then switched to home-services businesses when they came asking, and signed 6 customers in about 2 weeks *(medium, company's own post)* ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OH3-acrely-the-ai-customer-service-representative-for-home-services)). Your own September research ranked real estate the most crowded group *(our data)*. Solo realtors can still work, so build the month to find out fast.

| Where a small newcomer can win | Where it can't |
|---|---|
| Realtors with no CRM, or who ignore the one their brokerage gives them (54% of US agents use none) *(strong)* | Agents already on Zillow Pro, Lofty, BoldTrail or Sierra with AI turned on |
| Follow-up after the first reply (day 1 to day 30) across email and DMs, sent with the owner's OK. No tool found does this for under $80 *(weak: based on not finding one)* | Teams that buy paid customer lists and ads (Ylopo, CINC, Structurely) |
| Toronto-area realtors who work in several languages and want Canadian rules handled *(weak: based on not finding a competitor)* | First replies for home-services businesses (Yelp/Hatch, Jobber, Podium) |
| Works with whatever source or CRM they already use *(weak: useful, but not a lasting edge)* | Any pitch that opens with "AI replies to your customers" |

**Watch Cloze closely.** It costs about $17–$21 a month *(weak; sources disagree)*, reads email and social messages, suggests who to follow up with each morning and writes drafts ([Cloze](https://ai.cloze.com/platform/real-estate-crm/)). Your one-line difference: *Cloze reminds you and drafts; FollowUp follows through with your OK and stops the moment the customer replies.* Whether Cloze also sends on its own is unverified. Your lasting edge would be a measured "customers brought back" number from real Toronto realtors, which no bundle can copy.

## Five changes for November, in order

**1. Turn the AI back on today, and protect it.** Top up the credits. Give tests their own budget or key so a test run can never switch off real customers again, and keep the credit alert. While you are in billing, check whether the "paying" account really shows in Stripe. Nothing else on this list matters while the product cannot reply, and real customers cost under $1 a week to serve *(our data)*.

**2. Set up the 9 businesses you already have, yourself, this week.** Call each one and do a 20-minute screen share. Connect every place their customers write (Gmail, Instagram, website form), bring in their old customers, and send the first approved reply together while you are on the call. Stripe's founders called this "give me your laptop": they set the product up on the spot rather than sending a link *(medium)* ([Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/stripe-doing-things-that-dont-scale)). Paul Graham says nearly all startups have to recruit their first users by hand *(strong)* ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/paul-graham-offers-counter-intuitive-advice-to-startups-do-things-that-dont-scale/articleshow/21086175.cms)). For the first month, read every draft yourself each day and tell the realtor you do. That is a trust feature, and it gives the realtor who wants "fully automatic" a safe version of it. This step also fixes the disconnected-Gmail gap. Once the realtor you know sees a result, ask him for three introductions.

**3. Talk to 10–15 new realtors a week, all in one narrow group.** The group: solo or small-team realtors in the Toronto area whose customers arrive by Gmail and Instagram. Channels, in order: introductions from realtors already using it; one small brokerage or team leader (brokerages pay for the CRM for 36% of US agents *(strong)* ([NAR 2025 survey](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf))); short personal Instagram messages; and answering questions in agent Facebook groups, which is how Follow Up Boss found its early customers *(weak)* ([podcast summary](https://podpulse.ai/podcast-notes-and-takeaways/my-first-million-24m-in-one-day)). Count calls and meetings, not messages sent. One estimate says landing 10 paying B2B customers takes 50–150 real conversations *(weak, one source)* ([echai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/first-customers/how-many-people-should-i-actually-reach-out-to-before-i-expect-to-close-my-first-10-paying-customers)). 10–15 a week is a floor *(my estimate)*.

**4. Charge $39 from day one, with a written 30-day promise.** No free trials without an end date. A sample offer: "I set it up with you today and check every message for 30 days. If FollowUp hasn't answered at least [N] of your real customers within [X] minutes by day 30, I refund the month." Taking the card at the start matters: trials that ask for a card up front convert about 31–49% of the time, against 9–18% without one *(weak to medium, vendor benchmarks)* ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/); [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-benchmarks/)). Free trials with no deadline rarely convert *(weak)* ([BVP](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/design-partners-the-pre-launch-edge-most-ai-founders-ignore)). Offer the first 10 a price locked for 12 months in return for a 10-minute weekly check-in and one introduction *(my estimate)*. Your own rule says the lawyer reads the privacy policy before you charge, so book that review this week.

**5. Sell "follow-up with your OK," not "AI replies."** Open with three things: every customer from Gmail, Instagram and your website in one list; nothing goes out without your OK until you choose a rule; follow-ups stop the moment the customer answers. In Instagram and Messenger chats, ask for the customer's email or phone inside the first 24 hours, so the day-3 to day-30 follow-ups can go by email (see Meta's rule below). If that ask isn't in the product yet, it is the one product change worth making this month.

## Five rules that reshape the plan

None of these rules stops FollowUp, but each one changes how the plan has to work. A Canadian lawyer should confirm the CASL, privacy and RECO points before you scale.

| Rule | What it says | Risk to the plan | Do this |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google's Gmail check (CASA) | Apps that read Gmail need an outside security check every year. Until then: 100 users ever, plus a warning screen | Medium on timing, low on cost | Book the cheapest lab this month. Check your user count in Google Cloud |
| Meta's 24-hour rule | Automatic Instagram or Messenger messages only within 24 hours of the customer's last message. After that, only a person, for up to 7 days | Certain: it is policy | Ask for email or phone on day 1, then follow up by email |
| Canada's spam law (CASL) | An inquiry allows messages for 6 months. Every message names the sender, gives an address and has an unsubscribe | Low if built into the product | Record when each customer wrote, stop at 6 months unless they say yes, and put a footer on every email |
| RECO / TRESA (Ontario) | The realtor and brokerage answer for what AI writes, and the brokerage must be named exactly | Low, but brokerages will ask questions | Brokerage name in every signature, and a log of who approved what |
| Trust in AI replies | People accept AI when it is honest and a human is easy to reach | Medium: it decides who signs up | Approve-first by default, and never pretend to be human |

**Google's Gmail check (CASA): cheap, slow, and not a November blocker.** Google estimates **about 6 weeks** to review an app that reads Gmail *(strong)* ([Google FAQ](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817?hl=en)). The outside lab adds 1–5 weeks *(weak)* ([Agentic Fabriq](https://www.agenticfabriq.com/blog/google-oauth-verification-casa)). Together that is 7–11 weeks, and your own goal of "Google verified by Jan 4, 2027" is about 9 weeks away, so it only holds if the lab starts in the next 1–2 weeks. The cost is small: TAC Security's basic check is listed at **about US$540 a year**, with other pages showing $720–$855 *(medium; the vendor's pages disagree)* ([TAC Security](https://tacsecurity.com/google-casa-cloud-application-security-assessment/)). The $50,000+ figures you may see are outdated *(weak)* ([Bright Softwares](https://bright-softwares.com/blog/en/google-workspace/the-50000-gmail-add-on-myth-what-google-s-casa-certification-really-costs)). The check must be repeated every 12 months *(strong)* ([Google](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463816)). Until you pass, the app can **never have more than 100 users in total, and the cap cannot be reset** *(strong)* ([Google](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)). That is plenty for November, but a hard ceiling after it. One Google rule matters a lot for an AI product: Gmail data must never be used to train general AI models, and Google closes the review if it is *(strong)* ([Google](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13805798)). So keep your AI provider on no-training terms and say so in the privacy policy. A backup that avoids the check: realtors forward customer emails to FollowUp, and FollowUp only sends. Google's own page lists that send-only permission as both lower-risk and restricted, so check it in your console first *(strong source that contradicts itself)* ([Google](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13807380?hl=en)). In November, the warning screen matters more than the cap, and on a setup call you can explain it in one sentence.

**Meta's 24-hour rule means DM follow-ups move to email.** Meta allows automatic messages only within 24 hours of the customer's last message (up to 7 days when the chat began from a click-to-message ad) *(strong)* ([Meta policy](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/policy/)). After that, only a person may reply, using Meta's "human agent" label within 7 days *(strong)* ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/overview)), and that label is not allowed for automated messages *(medium)* ([Outstand](https://www.outstand.so/blog/instagram-dm-api)). FollowUp already follows this: the next-day DM goes at about 20 hours, and nothing automatic goes out after 24 *(our data)*. One open question no source settles: does an owner pressing "approve" on an AI-written draft count as a human reply? In review videos, show the person editing. Meta reportedly retired "Access Verification" on Oct 9, 2026 *(weak to medium; two outlets agree, Meta's own post not read)* ([Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-removes-access-verification-for-developers/832678)). Check the App Dashboard this week, because your December deadline may no longer apply. Each App Review round reportedly takes about 20 days *(weak)* ([Saurabh Dhar](https://www.saurabhdhar.com/blog/meta-app-review-20-days-2026-what-changed)), so one rejection costs about three weeks. That is another reason not to wait on it.

**Canada's spam law (CASL) fits your follow-up plan if the product enforces it.** A customer's inquiry gives implied consent to message them for **6 months**, and for 2 years after a purchase *(strong)* ([CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm)). A day-30 follow-up is well inside that. Every message needs the sender's name, a mailing address, a contact method and a working unsubscribe *(medium)* ([SendCheckIt](https://sendcheckit.com/blog/casl-compliance-guide)), and unsubscribes must be honoured within 10 business days *(medium)* ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/2015-12-7-major-media-company-pays-c200000-under-canadas-anti-spam-law/)). Your "STOP stops everything" rule already does better than that. Maximum fines are $1M per violation for a person and $10M for a company, but reported cases are rare and mostly old *(medium)* ([CCCC](https://cccc.org/news_blogs/legal/2015/08/07/casl-one-year-later)). Guidance from the realtor associations says following up a *referral* needs express consent *(medium to weak)* ([CREA-derived guidance](https://www.interiorrealtors.ca/matrix/documents/guidance_anti-spam_legislation.pdf)). Still unknown, so ask a lawyer: whether FollowUp shares responsibility with the realtor, and whether Instagram DMs count under the law. The same law also covers your own outreach messages to realtors. In the US, stay off text messages until you can record consent. Texting is the most expensive legal mistake available there, even after a 2025 court win for businesses *(medium)* ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/one-to-none-the-fcc-and-eleventh-circuit-issue-last-minute-rulings-gutting-the-one-to-one-tcpa-consent-rule.html)).

**RECO and TRESA put the realtor on the hook, so brokerages will ask questions.** RECO says using AI "doesn't change" a realtor's duties: the realtor and brokerage are responsible for what AI writes, must check it before sharing it, and should not put client data into public AI tools *(strong, read through a search extract)* ([RECO AI guidance](https://www.reco.on.ca/agents-and-brokerages/reco-bulletins/reco-guidance-use-of-artificial-intelligence/)). All advertising must name the brokerage clearly, exactly as registered *(strong)* ([RECO Bulletin 5.1](https://www.reco.on.ca/getmedia/fc6d0358-a8a1-4e6b-bfaa-2b8c312e1c3b/RECO-Bulletin-5-1-Advertising-requirements.pdf)), and the brokerage must supervise its agents *(strong)* ([RECO Bulletin 1.2](https://www.reco.on.ca/getmedia/d56aa6d5-63de-4786-8c68-08a638eeeaa7/RECO-Bulletin-1-2-Duty-to-comply-with-legislation.pdf)). No RECO rule requiring realtors to disclose AI use in messages was found. Whether a one-to-one follow-up counts as "advertising" is unclear, so always include the brokerage name. In practice, the gate is a brokerage's compliance person, who will ask three things: is the brokerage named, who approved what and when, and where does client data go? Answer all three in the product (brokerage name in every signature, a log of approvals, a data page) and they become a selling point. Under Canada's privacy law, data you process for realtors must be protected to the same standard *(medium)* ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/privacy-protection/1418134/canadian-privacy-regulators-weigh-in-on-how-to-comply-with-privacy-laws-when-using-generative-ai-systems)), which is one more reason for no-training AI terms.

**Trust favours "nothing goes out without your OK."** In Five9's survey, 75% of consumers prefer a human, and trust in AI to fix a problem is **55% when a human is easy to reach versus 26% when not** *(medium)* ([Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/clear-path-to-live-agent-boosts-trust-ai-customer-service/825835/)). Home buyers' trust in AI to help find a home fell from 30% in 2025 to 16% in 2026 *(medium)* ([Inman on Cotality](https://www.inman.com/2026/04/21/homebuyer-trust-in-ai-is-slipping-survey-finds-video/)). Agents' own top AI worries are accuracy (63%) and legal trouble (49%) *(medium)* ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/technology/youve-tried-ai-but-can-you-trust-it)). The one realtor you interviewed wants it "fully automatic" *(our data, one person)*. The way through: approve-first by default, automatic sending earned rule by rule, FollowUp never claims to be human and answers "is this a bot?" truthfully, and messages after day 7 bring something useful (a matching listing, an answer) rather than "just checking in." A vendor line like "buyers didn't realize they were texting a bot" would hurt you, not help.

## Conclusion

FollowUp's scarce resource isn't AI quality. Internal tests already pass at high rates. What it lacks is realtors who connect their accounts and open the app. That means the next big improvement will come from a phone call, not a code change, and the founder's calendar is now a bigger risk than Zillow, Meta or Google. The rules point the same way: Meta's 24-hour limit, CASL's six-month window and RECO's accountability all favour a product that follows up by email with the owner's OK and keeps a clear record. That is the product you already have. It just isn't being used yet.

November is built to produce the one thing no bundle can copy: a measured "customers brought back" number from named Toronto realtors. That number is your sales pitch, your answer to Cloze and Zillow Pro, and your keep-going signal. If the effort is real (40+ conversations, setups done by hand) and the number stays near zero, that is useful too. It tells you to change who FollowUp is for, not to keep polishing what it is.
