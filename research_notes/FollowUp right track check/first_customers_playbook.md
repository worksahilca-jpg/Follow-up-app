**Date:** 2026-11-01. **Scope:** how comparable vertical SMB / real-estate SaaS got their first 10–50 customers; concierge
work for AI products; paid vs free pilots; activation/retention benchmarks; realtor-specific channels.
**Method and limits:** internal notes read first (`followup/research/market/2026-09-10-first-paying-customers-gta.md`,
`followup/research/customers/2026-10-20-first-realtor-interview.md`,
`followup/research/customers/2026-10-04-does-the-solo-owner-have-this-problem.md`). External facts from WebSearch only.
**WebFetch is blocked in this sandbox** (lennysnewsletter.com and first-mrr.com failed DNS), so every external claim
below is from search-result snippets or search-engine summaries, not a fetched page. Re-verify any number before it

**Evidence grades (house style from the 2026-09-10 note):** A = primary source or large independent sample.

podcast summary, aggregator. D = inference in this note.

**FollowUp's own numbers used throughout (supplied by the founder, as of 2026-11-01):** 9 outside businesses signed up
since September, 1 in the last 30 days; 5 end-customers (leads) captured in 30 days across 3 businesses; 1 lead answered
through FollowUp in 30 days; 0 bookings; about 1 paying. Price $39 / $79 per month.

## Q1. How did comparable vertical SMB / real-estate SaaS companies get their first 10–50 customers?

### Takeaway
Every case found used founder-led, high-touch selling into one narrow segment, usually one the founders had a personal
or community connection to, and most charged from the first customer. It was slow: Jobber took 6+ months to find a first
customer and had 3 paying customers after a year; Follow Up Boss's early traction is described as "glacial." FollowUp
having about 1 paying customer roughly two months after its first outside signups is not off-track against these
pre-AI histories. The gap is elsewhere: the product's core action (answering a lead) happened once in 30 days (see Q4).

### Cited Findings

**Follow Up Boss (real-estate CRM, founded 2011, bootstrapped)** — the closest analogue to FollowUp's buyer
- The idea reportedly came from reading complaints about real-estate software in agent Facebook groups, and early
  customers came from the CEO answering questions in those groups, not from ads. People who looked at his profile found
  the product. [C — podcast summaries of My First Million] — [podpulse summary](https://podpulse.ai/podcast-notes-and-takeaways/my-first-million-24m-in-one-day); [first-mrr study (search snippet)](https://first-mrr.com/study/follow-up-boss)
- First customer paid **$150/month**; early traction described as "glacial." [C — third-party summary] — [podpulse](https://podpulse.ai/podcast/my-first-million/my-first-million-24m-in-one-day)
- About **four years to ~$100k monthly revenue, with 11 employees**. [C — third-party summary, not the founder's own words] — [podpulse](https://podpulse.ai/podcast-notes-and-takeaways/my-first-million-24m-in-one-day)
- The founders built for six months and "weren't gaining traction" before joining a peer group (The Software
  Roundtable). [C — podcast show notes] — [The Foundation podcast notes](https://thefoundation.libsyn.com/webpage/2016/12/28)
- Exit reported at $500M ($400M cash + $100M earnout) after ~12 years bootstrapped; revenue estimates conflict
  ($24.4M per Latka 2023 vs ~$28M per a podcast summary). [C] — [first-mrr snippet](https://first-mrr.com/study/follow-up-boss); [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/follow-up-boss)

**Jobber (field-service software, Edmonton, ~2010–11)** — closest analogue on price and founder geography
- The co-founder says they spent **more than 6 months** going to small businesses every day to find the first customer;
  a summary says he heard "no" from every prospect in that period. [B — co-founder in podcast transcript] — [Agents of Scale transcript](https://agentsofscale.transistor.fm/6/transcript)
- First customer: a local painting business; the founders worked with a painting-business owner who was a close friend
  and "living those pain points daily." [B/C — interview write-ups] — [Medium interview](https://medium.com/@geneswank/living-on-the-bleeding-edge-sam-pillar-of-jobber-f27dcbffb5f1); [AccessAlberta interview](https://joanne-fedeyko.squarespace.com/blog/2021-2-23-accessalberta-interview-series-jobber-ceo-sam-pillar)
- **One year in: three customers paying $29/month.** Early product did only scheduling and invoicing; the CEO recalls
  "maybe 20 or 30 customers" in the first period. [C — secondary write-up] — [Network Effects](https://network-effects.beehiiv.com/p/jobber-the-operating-system-for-local-service-pros)

**Podium (reviews → messaging for local businesses, Utah, 2014)**
- For about **nine months** the two founders sold **door-to-door** to businesses in Utah County and Salt Lake County by day
  and learned to code at night; the CEO later credited door-to-door as a key to success. [B — founder interviews] — [Seed Stage Stories](https://medium.com/seed-stage-stories/eric-rea-building-a-3b-company-from-the-attic-of-a-bike-shop-695fbe7034f4); [Utah.gov "In Their Words"](https://business.utah.gov/in-their-words/in-their-words-eric-rea)
- The product started from helping the CEO's father's tire shop get reviews; that shop (or a Utah auto shop — sources
  differ) was among the first customers. Target was single-location small businesses. [B] — [Business Insider India](https://businessinsider.in/the-ceo-of-podium-helped-his-dad-jazz-up-his-tire-shops-online-presence-he-ended-up-launching-a-cloud-startup-backed-by-accel-and-gv-which-now-has-more-than-37000-business-customers-/amp_articleshow/69470190.cms)
- When door-to-door stopped scaling they hired a salesperson experienced in selling software by phone. [B] — [Seed Stage Stories](https://medium.com/seed-stage-stories/eric-rea-building-a-3b-company-from-the-attic-of-a-bike-shop-695fbe7034f4)

**ServiceTitan (home-services software, LA, founded 2012)**
- Both founders' families ran trades businesses; the company started narrow (residential plumbing, and per one source
  garage-door repair) and only moved to HVAC after it "could deliver incredible ROI" there with customers who loved it. [B/C] — [Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/servicetitan-powering-americas-home-heroes/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/servicetitan)
- A manager at Dutton Plumbing is described as one of the first customers; word then spread through the LA Armenian
  immigrant business community that the founders had a tool that eased everyday operating problems. [B/C] — [Built In LA](https://www.builtinla.com/articles/silicon-hills-servicetitans-humble-origins); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/servicetitan)

**Housecall Pro (home services, ~2013)**
- Co-founder credits **ride-alongs** with technicians for most early features and says they "stayed really close with

[C — interview snippets] — [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/leadership/housecall-pro-is-helping-home-service-pros-grow-their/390848); [RYNO podcast](https://rynoss.com/podcast/episode-86-transforming-the-trades-through-technology/)

**Structurely (AI inside-sales assistant for real estate, ~2016–17)** — the closest AI-for-realtor-follow-up analogue
- First product "automated the initial follow-up with new leads," saving realtors time. [B — economic-development profile of the founders] — [DSM Partnership](https://www.dsmpartnership.com/news-media/blog/five-lessons-learned-from-andrew-dickelman-and-nate-joens-co-founders-of-structurely)
- The company has said its platform is **"ideal for real estate teams, vs. the individual agent"**; published case studies
  are a Keller Williams team, eXp Realty and a BHGRE brokerage. One source mentions a rollout plan to 3,500 agents in six
  monthly waves (single source, unverified). [B/C] — [KW team case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/structurely/case-study/the-deberry-team-of-keller-williams-customer-case-study); [eXp case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/structurely/case-study/outsourcing-lead-follow-up-using-ai); [BHGRE case study](https://structurely.com/blogs/humanisa-vs-aiisa-bhgre-case-study); [DSM Partnership](https://dsmpartnership.com/growing-business-here/business-resources/small-business-resources/a-personal-touch-)

**Acrely (YC S25, voice AI) — a 2025 vertical-AI data point that moved *away* from realtors**
- Per its YC launch post, Acrely first built voice-AI scheduling **for real estate agents**; home-services businesses came
  inbound asking for it, the team pivoted, and landed **6 customers in about 2 weeks**. Its YC listing now describes
  enterprise voice agents. [B — company's own launch post, via snippet] — [YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OH3-acrely-the-ai-customer-service-representative-for-home-services); [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/acrely)

**Cross-company research (Lenny Rachitsky's first-10-customers series)**
- Lenny's series on how Figma, Stripe, Airtable, Shopify, Carta, Gusto and others got their first ten customers found
  network-first sourcing dominant (Gusto: "friends we knew who were just starting their businesses"; Amplitude: YC
  network). [C — via summaries; original paywalled/unfetched] — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-businesses); [echai summary](https://echai.ventures/startingup/distribution-channels/how-do-the-fastest-growing-b2b-companies-find-and-scale-their-distribution)
- The companion post recommends starting with your personal network "looking for people who match your ICP"; cold
  outbound (emails, DMs, cold calls) was called one of the recurring surprises across interviews. [C] — [Lenny: How to find and win your first 10 B2B customers](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-win-your-first-10-b2b-customers); [Pascal's notes](https://pascalsnotes.substack.com/p/cold-outbound-is-under-appreciated)
- Counter-example: one enterprise startup reports hundreds of cold emails and LinkedIn DMs converted none of its first ten.
  [D-level anecdote] — [lavx](https://news.lavx.hu/article/beyond-cold-outreach-how-an-enterprise-auth-startup-acquired-its-first-10-customers)

### Inferences
- **Common pattern across all cases [D]:** (1) one narrow sub-segment first; (2) a personal or community connection
  to it (father's tire shop, fathers' plumbing firms, a friend's painting company, a co-founder's house-painter father);
  (3) months of in-person or one-to-one selling; (4) word of mouth inside a tight community (ServiceTitan's Armenian
  business network; Follow Up Boss's agent Facebook groups); (5) charging from the first customer (Jobber $29, Follow Up
  Boss $150).
- **Timeline calibration [D]:** Jobber (3 paying after 12 months) and Follow Up Boss ("glacial") suggest that ~1 paying
  customer two months after first outside signups is normal for a pre-AI SMB tool. AI-era companies can move faster when
  demand is inbound (Acrely, 6 in 2 weeks), which is a reason to watch where inbound interest comes from rather than
  forcing the chosen segment.
- **Real estate caution [D]:** Structurely positions itself for teams, not individual agents; Acrely moved from agents to
  home services when demand came from there; and FollowUp's own 2026-09-10 note had declined real estate as the most
  crowded segment. None of this says solo realtors cannot work, but the evidence that solo agents are an easy first
  buyer is thin. The 30-day test below should be built to falsify it quickly.
- **What to copy now [D]:** the Follow Up Boss pattern (be useful in the agent communities where complaints are posted,
  let the profile do the selling) plus the Jobber/Podium pattern (go to the agent in person or on a call and set it up
  with them) — not broad signup marketing.

### Gaps
- No primary account found of how Ylopo (founded ~2013 per [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/profile/ylopo)),
  Lofty/Chime, Hatch, Bland or Synthflow got their first customers. Search returned profiles and marketing pages only.
- No first-hand Follow Up Boss transcript was retrieved; the $150 first customer and ~4 years to ~$100k MRR are from
  third-party podcast summaries.
- Lenny's actual percentages (how many companies used network vs cold vs community) were not retrievable (paywall/fetch
  blocked). A marketing page attributing "60% of B2B SaaS early growth to founder networks" to Bessemer could not be
  traced and is not used.

## Q2. Does the concierge / "Wizard of Oz" approach (founder manually doing the work) work for AI products?

### Takeaway
Doing things by hand for the first customers is the most-cited early-stage advice (Paul Graham's "Do Things That Don't
Scale," Stripe's "Collison installation"), and the vertical-SMB winners did versions of it (Housecall Pro ride-alongs,
Podium door-to-door). For AI products the specific form is: set the customer up yourself, sit in the loop on every
AI output until accuracy is proven, and log every failure. The one firm rule: never claim automation you are faking.

### Cited Findings
- Paul Graham (July 2013) tells founders to recruit early users manually even when it "seems tedious and inefficient."
  [A — primary essay; quoted via secondary] — [paulgraham.com/ds.html](http://paulgraham.com/ds.html); [Business Insider summary](https://www.businessinsider.in/paul-graham-offers-counter-intuitive-advice-to-startups-do-things-that-dont-scale/articleshow/21086175.cms)
- The **"Collison installation"**: instead of "we'll send you a link," Stripe's founders said "Right then, give me your
  laptop" and set the prospect up on the spot. [A/B — Graham's essay, quoted secondhand] — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/stripe-doing-things-that-dont-scale)
- Lenny's research also highlights Stripe's habit of installing on the prospect's laptop rather than sending a link. [C — summary] — [echai summary of Lenny](https://echai.ventures/startingup/distribution-channels/how-do-the-fastest-growing-b2b-companies-find-and-scale-their-distribution)
- Concierge MVP definition: deliver the outcome manually as a hands-on service to a small number of real customers, who
  know no full product exists yet; classic examples are DoorDash founders delivering food and Airbnb founders
  photographing listings. [C — explainers] — [Kromatic Real Startup Book](https://kromatic.com/real-startup-book/3-generative-product-research/concierge-test/); [echai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/validate-idea/what-is-a-concierge-or-wizard-of-oz-mvp-and-when-should-i-use-one)
- AI-specific version: "act as the agent," transcribe every pilot, log every hallucination, keep humans in the loop until
  accuracy is stable. [C — practitioner blog] — [EONSR](https://eonsr.com/ai-concierge-tests-do-things-that-dont-scale)
- Limit: you can do things manually first, but you cannot claim AI and then do it by hand; Engineer.ai is cited as a
  company that faced legal trouble over this. [C] — [echai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/validate-idea/what-is-a-concierge-or-wizard-of-oz-mvp-and-when-should-i-use-one)
- Limit: concierge is for learning, not growth; serving too many by hand leads to burnout and diluted learning. [C] — [causo guide](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/concierge-mvp-deliver-manually-before-building-2026)
- Vertical SMB precedent: Housecall Pro's ride-alongs produced most early features; Jobber's first version was built with
  a painting-business owner living the pain daily. [B/C] — [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/leadership/housecall-pro-is-helping-home-service-pros-grow-their/390848); [AccessAlberta](https://joanne-fedeyko.squarespace.com/blog/2021-2-23-accessalberta-interview-series-jobber-ceo-sam-pillar)

### Inferences
- **FollowUp-specific concierge [D]:** the realtor interviewed on 2026-10-20 said his biggest pain is ~699 old customers in
  his GoHighLevel pipeline nobody is writing to, and his main concern is that it must be "fully automatic." A
  concierge pilot that fits both: the founder personally imports the CSV, reviews the first drafts for that agent every
  day (FollowUp already releases old GHL conversations about 10 a day as drafts), fixes the style/qualifying order by
  hand, and reports back weekly what was sent and who replied. The agent experiences "fully automatic"; the founder is
  the safety layer. This must be described honestly ("I personally check every message in your first month"), which
  is a trust feature, not a confession.
- **Collison install for the existing 9 signups [D]:** 8 of 9 have no answered lead through FollowUp in 30 days. The
  cheapest next step is a 20-minute screen-share with each to connect every channel they actually use, import their
  old leads, and send the first approved reply while on the call. This is copyable today and needs no new code.
- **What to log during concierge [D]:** every draft the owner edited or rejected (and why), every lead the product
  missed, time from lead arrival to first reply. That log is the activation evidence and the roadmap.

### Gaps
- No quantified evidence found that concierge-first AI startups convert or retain better than self-serve ones; the
  support is precedent and expert advice, not a dataset.
- No primary source found for a 2023–2026 YC vertical-AI company describing its concierge phase in detail.

## Q3. Paid vs free pilots; charging from day one; pricing for first customers

### Takeaway
The weight of advice (YC partners, VC design-partner guidance) is: charge something from day one, and if you pilot, make
it short, with written success criteria, an end date and a binary paid/not-paid decision. Free pilots fail mainly from
having no deadline. Comparable SMB tools charged their very first customers ($29/month at Jobber, $150/month at Follow Up
Boss). No source gives a "right" founder-deal price for $39–$79 SMB tools; that part is inference.

### Cited Findings
- YC group partners (summarized): paying customers give more candid feedback than free users; free can be legitimate only
  with an intentional plan to get paid later. [C — summaries of a YC video] — [summify](https://summify.io/discover/turning-your-users-into-paying-customers-9pQJXR/); [rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/ycombinator/turning-your-users-into-paying-customers)
- YC partner Tom Blomfield is cited as saying a paid pilot with clear success criteria is real proof, while a long,
  undefined free design partnership proves little. [C — secondary attribution] — [echai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/first-customers/should-i-charge-my-very-first-customers-or-give-it-away-free-to-get-traction)
- Pilot structure advice: defined end date, success metrics, and credit the pilot fee toward the full contract. [C] — [echai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/founder-sales/should-i-offer-a-free-trial-a-paid-pilot-or-a-discount-to-close-my-first-customers); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/pilot-to-paid-conversion-startups)
- Free pilots tend to fail because they lack an end date and success criteria, not because of the product. [C] — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/pilot-to-paid-conversion-startups)
- Free design-partner pilots are "unlikely to convert" without a hard deadline and binary ask. [C — VC blog; from the 2026-09-10 internal note] — [BVP Atlas](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/design-partners-the-pre-launch-edge-most-ai-founders-ignore)
- Even a nominal or discounted charge changes engagement. [C] — [Standard Ledger](https://www.standardledger.co/uk/article/from-pilot-to-paid-how-to-stop-giving-your-product-away-for-free/); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/startup-sales-how-to-get-pilot-customers-to-pay-9214bf105a9a)
- Trial-to-paid: opt-in (no card) ~8.9%–18.2%; opt-out (card required) ~31%–49%. [B/C — ChartMogul and First Page Sage via the 2026-09-10 internal note] — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/); [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-benchmarks/)
- First-customer prices at analogues: Jobber $29/month (3 customers at one year) — [Network Effects](https://network-effects.beehiiv.com/p/jobber-the-operating-system-for-local-service-pros); Follow Up Boss $150/month first customer — [podpulse](https://podpulse.ai/podcast/my-first-million/my-first-million-24m-in-one-day). [C]
- What realtors already pay: the largest share of US agents (34%) spend **$50–$250/month** on technology; 24% spend more
  than $500/month. [A — NAR 2025 Technology Survey, random sample of 49,233 invited, 1,241 responses; US] — [NAR survey PDF](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf); [Seattle Agent Magazine](https://seattleagentmagazine.com/2025/09/19/nar-technology-survey/)
- SMB AI voice agents are priced around $97–$900/month in a 2025 buyer's guide; overage bills are cited as a pain point.
  [C] — [Ryseflow guide](https://ryseflowai.beehiiv.com/p/ai-voice-agents-2025-guide)

### Inferences
- **Price is not the bottleneck [D]:** $39 sits below the $50–$250 band where the largest share of US agents spend, and
  below every AI-agent comparable. With 1 answered lead in 30 days, nobody has yet seen enough value to test price.
  Fix activation first; do not discount further.
- **Recommended founder deal (copyable, D):** charge from day one at the list price, with a written 30-day success
  promise instead of a free pilot. Example: "$39 this month. I set it up with you today and check every message for 30
  days. If by day 30 FollowUp hasn't answered at least [N] of your real leads within [X] minutes and brought back at least
  one old customer into a conversation, I refund the month." Card on file at the start puts conversion in the opt-out
  band (31–49%) rather than opt-in (9–18%).
- **Founding-customer lock [D]:** offer the first 10 paying agents the current price locked for 12–24 months in exchange
  for a weekly 10-minute check-in and a referral if it works. This trades price for learning and word of mouth, which is
  how Follow Up Boss and ServiceTitan grew.
- **If a free pilot is unavoidable [D]:** 14 days, success criteria written before connecting, card at day 10, decision
  on a calendar date. Never open-ended.

### Gaps
- No primary YC document found stating "charge from day one" as policy; YC attributions above are from third-party
  summaries.
- No data on whether money-back guarantees or price locks improve conversion for $30–$80/month SMB tools.
- No Canadian (CREA/TRREB) equivalent of the NAR tech-spend figures found.

## Q4. What do good activation and retention look like at this stage? What does "good" look like for 10 users?

### Takeaway
Published benchmarks are mostly vendor-sourced and disagree, but they cluster: ~37% of signups reach activation on
average (higher in CRM/sales and AI tools); good SMB SaaS keeps ~60% of users at 6 months; good SMB monthly churn is
~2.5–5%; and ~40% of active users saying they would be "very disappointed" without the product is the common
product-market-fit bar. At 10 users these percentages become head-counts, not statistics. FollowUp's own data shows its
value moment (a real lead answered) happened for at most 1 of 9 signups in 30 days, far below the activation average.

### Cited Findings
**Activation**
- Average SaaS activation rate **37.5%** (median 37%) across 62 B2B SaaS companies; by vertical, **CRM & Sales 42.6%**,
  **AI & ML 54.8%**, MarTech 24% (547 companies). [C — vendor benchmark report] — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/activation-metrics-saas/)
- Average time-to-value ~1 day 12 hours (62 companies); another Userpilot page shows ~1 day 2 hours. [C — conflicting versions] — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/activation-metrics-saas/)
- A 2026 compilation claims benchmarks from 560+ companies and stresses that medians swing widely by category. [C] — [Artisan Growth Strategies](https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/blog/saas-activation-rate-benchmarks-2026)

**Retention and churn**
- Lenny Rachitsky's crowdsourced benchmark: **SMB/mid-market SaaS user retention at 6 months ~60% = good, ~80% = great**;
  net revenue retention ~90% good, ~110% great. [C — practitioner survey, via secondary write-ups] — [Casey Winters / Lenny study](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/what-is-good-retention-an-exhaustive-benchmark-study-with-lenny-rachitsky); [thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1270358817679671296.html)
- Lenny on monthly churn, **B2B SMB + mid-market: 2.5%–5% = good, <1.5% = great**; revenue retention matters more than
  customer retention in SMB. [C — mirror of Lenny's post] — [mirror of "What is good monthly churn"](https://personal-learnings.pages.dev/reading_room/issues/lennys_newsletter/0510_what-is-good-monthly-churn.html)
- SMB SaaS logo churn **3–7% per month** is the commonly repeated planning band (≈31–58% a year). [C] — [Founderpath calculator](https://founderpath.com/free-tools/churn-rate-calculator)
- A 2026 study of 38 SaaS clients / 184,000 customer-months reports **median monthly churn 4.7%** and **SMB annual logo churn
  31%** (enterprise 8%). [C — single vendor study] — [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-churn-benchmarks-2026)
- SaaS Capital publishes primary churn benchmarks for B2B SaaS (figures not retrieved in this pass). — [SaaS Capital](https://www.saas-capital.com/research/churn-benchmarks-for-b2b-saas-companies/)

**Engagement and product-market fit**
- B2B SaaS stickiness (DAU/MAU) median ~13% attributed to Mixpanel's 2022 benchmarks (secondhand); for weekly-use tools
  like CRMs, WAU/MAU is the better yardstick, ~20–40% typical, 45%+ high. [C — conflicting secondary sources] — [Gainsight](https://www.gainsight.com/product-management-metrics/dau-mau/); [Revos](https://www.revos.ai/blog/wau-mau)
- Sean Ellis test: if ≥40% of the right users say "very disappointed" if they could no longer use the product, fit is
  likely; ask only active users (e.g., used in the last 2 weeks). [C] — [Learning Loop](https://learningloop.io/plays/product-market-fit-survey)
- Superhuman's first Ellis score was **22%**, below the 40% bar, before it segmented users and improved. [B — First Round Review, via mirror] — [First Round Review mirror](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/articles/review.firstround.com/superhuman-product-market-fit-engine)

**Volume context from internal notes**
- A solo realtor typically gets ~10–50 inquiries a month; one realtor in FollowUp's own data had 16–22 customers after a
  few weeks on Gmail. [C/internal] — `followup/research/customers/2026-10-04-does-the-solo-owner-have-this-problem.md`

### Inferences
**FollowUp against the benchmarks [D, using the founder's numbers]:**

| Step | FollowUp, last 30 days | Benchmark | Read |

| Bookings | 0 | — | No outcome evidence to sell on yet |

- **Define activation as an outcome, not a setup step [D]:** "a real lead answered through FollowUp within 24 hours of
  connecting" (or "an old customer replied to a FollowUp message"). Track per business, weekly.
- **What "good" looks like for 10 users (head-counts, D):**
  - Activation: ≥6 of 10 businesses have a real lead answered through FollowUp in their first 7 days (above the ~37–43%
    average, appropriate for a founder-installed product).
  - Weekly use: ≥6 of 10 businesses approve or auto-send at least one reply in any given week (roughly the 60% WAU-style
    bar for weekly tools).
  - Monthly retention: lose no more than 1 paying business in a quarter (≈3% a month, inside the 2.5–5% "good" band).
  - 6-month retention: ≥6 of 10 still active (Lenny's "good" for SMB).
  - Ellis test at ~day 45: ≥4 of 10 active owners say "very disappointed."
  - Outcome: ≥1 booking or reactivated old customer per business per month that the owner attributes to FollowUp; this is
    the testimonial and the referral trigger.
- **At n=10, one business = 10 points [D].** Do not read churn or activation percentages as signal; read the per-business
  log (who answered, who didn't, why). The Superhuman example shows a low first score is normal if you segment and fix.

## Q5. Which channels work for realtors specifically?

### Takeaway
Three channels have real evidence: (1) agent Facebook communities, worked by being helpful rather than advertising
(Follow Up Boss's main early channel; Lab Coat Agents grew from a Facebook group into a vendor-deals channel);
(2) brokerages and teams, which fund much of agents' tech (brokerages most often pay for CRM) and are where AI
follow-up incumbents sell; (3) social media, which agents themselves rank as their top lead source, so Instagram is
where they are reachable and where FollowUp's own Instagram-DM value is visible. Board-level partnerships (TRREB)
exist but are for established vendors, not a 30-day channel.

### Cited Findings
**Facebook groups / agent communities**
- Follow Up Boss built its early business through authentic engagement in real-estate Facebook groups, not Facebook ads.
  [C — podcast summary] — [podpulse](https://podpulse.ai/podcast-notes-and-takeaways/my-first-million-24m-in-one-day)
- Lab Coat Agents started as a Facebook group in October 2014, passed 10,000 members within about a year, and later
  profiles cite 137,000–157,000+ members (figures conflict). [B/C] — [Inman 2015](https://inman.com/2015/10/01/real-estate-facebook-group-unveils-lead-generation-tool); [Village profile](https://village.ai/company/labcoat-agents)
- Lab Coat Agents monetizes through vendor partnerships: a member deals page with offers such as waived or 50%-off setup
  fees (REDX, PowerISA, PropStream), and early on took equity to co-design vendor tools. [B/C] — [Inman 2015](https://inman.com/2015/10/01/real-estate-facebook-group-unveils-lead-generation-tool); [Placester](https://placester.com/?p=9249)

**Brokerages and teams**
- Two-thirds of US agents agree their brokerage provides all the technology tools they need. Brokerages most often pay for
  transaction management (39%), eSignature (37%) and **CRM (36%)**; agents most often pay themselves for social media (52%),
  cloud storage (48%) and virtual tours (47%). [A — NAR 2025 survey; US] — [NAR survey PDF](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf); [Seattle Agent Magazine](https://seattleagentmagazine.com/2025/09/19/nar-technology-survey/)
- Structurely's published wins are a KW team, eXp Realty and a BHGRE brokerage, and it calls its product ideal for teams
  rather than individual agents. [B] — [KW team](https://www.casestudies.com/company/structurely/case-study/the-deberry-team-of-keller-williams-customer-case-study); [eXp](https://www.casestudies.com/company/structurely/case-study/outsourcing-lead-follow-up-using-ai)

**Social media / lead sources**
- Agents rank **social media as their top lead source (39%)**, then CRM (23%), local MLS (17%) and brokerage website (13%);
  75% use social media as a tool; 21% use a CRM with AI-powered insights; among AI users, ChatGPT is most common (58%).
  [A — NAR 2025] — [NAR newsroom](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/realtors-embrace-ai-digital-tools-to-enhance-client-service-nar-survey-finds); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2025-technology-survey-realtor-tech-usage-trends/)

  [B — joint press release] — [Newswire.ca](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/canada-s-largest-real-estate-board-trreb-partners-with-skyslope-to-bring-digital-forms-library-to-over-70-000-members-810824717.html)

**Internal evidence**
- The one interviewed GTA realtor already uses GoHighLevel and an Instagram messaging tool built by someone he knows;
  his pain is a ~699-person old pipeline; he wants it "fully automatic." [internal, one person] — `followup/research/customers/2026-10-20-first-realtor-interview.md`

### Inferences
- **Channel ranking for the next 30 days [D]:**
  1. **Warm network and referrals from the realtor already engaged.** Lenny's research says network first; ServiceTitan
     and Follow Up Boss grew by word of mouth within one community. Ask the interviewed realtor for 3 introductions to
     agents in his office or circle once he sees one result.
  2. **One small independent brokerage or team lead.** Brokerages pay for CRM and incumbents sell to teams; one
     broker-owner who introduces FollowUp to 5–15 agents is worth more than 50 cold DMs. Offer the broker a free seat and
     a weekly results summary for agents who opt in.
  3. **Instagram DMs to active GTA agents** (agents' top lead source is social, so their attention is there and FollowUp
     can show its Instagram-reply value). Personal, specific, low volume (10–15 a day). Check CASL applicability to
     social DMs before scaling (the 2026-09-10 note covers CASL for email).
  4. **Agent Facebook groups, Follow Up Boss style:** answer questions about follow-up and lead response for 2–3 weeks
     before mentioning the product; share a real measured result once one exists.
  5. **Not now:** TRREB or other board partnerships (fit for established vendors), paid ads, broad signup campaigns.

#### Combined 30-day playbook (inference, Grade D)
Built from the patterns above; every number is a target to test, not a benchmark.

- **Days 1–7: rescue the 9 signups before finding new ones.** Call each; do a 20-minute "Collison install" on a
  screen-share: connect every channel they actually receive leads on, import old leads (CSV/GHL), send the first approved
  reply live. Goal: ≥5 of 9 have a real lead answered through FollowUp by day 7. Record why each of the others did not.
- **Days 1–30: concierge every active account.** Founder reviews each account's drafts daily, tells the owner so, logs
  every edit/rejection/miss. Weekly 10-minute check-in per account; ask the Ellis question at day ~30 for active users.
- **Days 3–30: add 10–15 new realtor conversations a week** from (1) referrals by the engaged realtor, (2) one
  brokerage/team lead, (3) personal Instagram DMs, (4) Facebook-group helpfulness. Target ~25–30 conversations → ~8–10
  installs → ~4–5 paying by day 30–45 (using the internal note's ~50% demo→pilot and ~35% pilot→paid assumptions).
- **Pricing:** paid from day one at list price with a written 30-day success promise and refund; founding-price lock for
  the first 10 in exchange for check-ins and one referral.
- **Weekly scorecard (one row per business):** channels connected; leads captured; leads answered through FollowUp and
  median time to answer; old customers who replied; bookings; owner edits/rejections; paid yes/no; referral asked/given.
- **Kill/adjust signal at day 30:** if fewer than 5 of ~10 installed realtors have a lead answered through FollowUp in
  their first week even with founder setup, the problem is channel coverage or fit, not marketing; check whether other
  segments (contractors, clinics) are coming inbound, as Acrely's home-services demand did.
